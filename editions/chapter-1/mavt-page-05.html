Sthiramati, Madhyāntavibhāgaṭīkā — Yamaguchi p. 5

OCR lines 1282–1318. Methodology: mavt-reedition-workflow.md. Tibetan witness: BO_T06_D4032:191a-16 → 191b-13.

Page 5 finishes the gloss on the homage-verse (MAVBh kārikā 1): asmadādibhyaḥ ("to us and the like"), abhyarhya (the verb), and the bhāṣya's second pāda yatiṣye 'rthavivecane ("I shall exert myself in the discrimination of the meaning") are all glossed in MS-attested Sanskrit; then a long block answers kimartham idaṃ śāstraṃ praṇītam? ("Why was this śāstra composed?") with four sequential answers — (a) to generate the Buddhas' samyak-nirvikalpa-jñāna (b) to refute two contradictory theses about dharma-nairātmya (c) to remove non-understanding and mis-understanding in the audience (alternate view) (d) to remove the bodhisattvas' faintheartedness in face of the five-aspected jñeya. The page ends with the pratīka of MAV I.2a: lakṣaṇaṃ hy āvṛtis tattvam.

This page is mostly MS-attested; the reconstructed portions are short and grammatical (rather than evidential) in nature.

§1 — ceti | … asmadādibhyaḥ (gloss of the kārikā dative) VERIFIED

Yamaguchi text (MS)

ceti | kebhyo vaktāram 'asmadādibhya iti vayam ādir yeṣāṁ te 'smadādayas tebhyo 'smadādibhyaḥ | (OCR 1284–1285)

Tibetan

སུ་ལ་འཆད་པ་ཞེ་ན། བདག་སོགས་ལ་ཞེས་བྱ་བ་སྟེ། བདག་ཐོག་མར་བྱས་པ་གང་ཡིན་པ་དེ་དག་ནི་བདག་ལ་སོགས་པ་སྟེ། — su la 'chad pa zhe na | bdag sogs la zhes bya ba ste … (BO_T06_D4032:191a-13)

No change

Clean MS gloss using bahuvrīhi analysis: "we (vayam) are first (ādiḥ) of whom (yeṣām), they are 'asmad-ādayaḥ', to them — 'asmadādibhyaḥ'". Tib mirrors verbatim.

Translation

"ca" — [the particle]. To whom is the expounder [worshipped]? "asmadādibhyaḥ" — i.e. those of whom we are the first, "we and the like" — to them, "asmadādibhyaḥ".

§2 — gloss of the kārikā by asmadādibhyaḥ PROPOSED

Yamaguchi text (mixed)

anenātmano 'mṛiṣāyā upadeśo bñāsamāno nirdiṣṭaḥ | (OCR 1285–1286)

Tibetan

འདི་ནི་བདག་ཉིད་ཀྱི་མི་སླུ་བ་ལས་ལུང་མནོས་པར་སྟོན་ཏོ། ། — 'di ni bdag nyid kyi mi slu ba las lung mnos par ston to (BO_T06_D4032:191a-13)

Two OCR slips to fix; one structural reinterpretation

The OCR's mṛiṣāyā with anomalous "ṛi" is just mṛṣāyāḥ ("of falsehood"), and with the negative prefix a- from preceding ātmano '—, it becomes amṛṣāyāḥ ("of [his] truthfulness", gen. sg. f.). The OCR's bñāsamāno is plainly bhāsamāno ("being manifest").

Structurally, the Tibetan 'di ni bdag nyid kyi mi slu ba las lung mnos par ston to parses as: "this [verse] shows that [Vasubandhu's] teaching has been heard from [a teacher of] truthfulness of [his] very own". The Skt would be anenātmano 'mṛṣāyā[ḥ sakāśād] upadeśo bhāsamāno nirdiṣṭaḥ — "by this, the teaching is shown as having been heard from a truthfulness which is his own". Yamaguchi's reading parses; the only real ambiguity is whether bhāsamāno = "manifest" (passive sense) or "[the teaching that] shines" (active middle). Both yield the same gloss in English.

Proposed reading

anenātmano 'mṛṣāyā upadeśo bhāsamāno nirdiṣṭaḥ |

Translation

By this [phrasing of the kārikā], [Vasubandhu] indicates a teaching that manifests as [coming] from his own truthfulness.

Yamaguchi text (MS)

abhyarhyety abhyarcya | abhitaḥ purataḥ sākṣād iva sthitam | arhitvā arcayitvā kāyavāṅmanobhiḥ | (OCR 1288–1289)

Tibetan

མངོན་མཆོད་ཅེས་བྱ་བ་ནི་རྗེད་པའོ། ། མངོན་པར་ཞེས་བྱ་བ་ནི་མདུན་ནམ་མངོན་སུམ་དང་འདྲ་བར་གནས་པའོ། ། མཆོད་པ་ནི་ལུས་དང་ངག་དང་ཡིད་ཀྱིས་རྗེད་པའོ། ། (BO_T06_D4032:191a-26)

No change

Verbatim correspondence between Skt and Tib glosses. abhitaḥ purataḥ sākṣād iva sthitam renders exactly Tib mdun nam mngon sum dang 'dra bar gnas pa'o. Note 1 (apparatus): Yamaguchi mentions that the MS placed abhyarhya…manobhiḥ at a different location (p. 3 n. 6); he moved it here on the basis of the Tibetan. This is the kind of editorial move that needs to be noted as Yamaguchi's choice — it remains correct given the Tib.

Translation

"abhyarhya" means "abhyarcya" (having paid homage). abhi-: "from in front, as if directly present". arhitvā = arcayitvā ("having honored") — with body, speech, and mind.

§5–§7 — yatiṣye 'rthavivecane gloss + saptamī as causal VERIFIED

Yamaguchi text (MS)

yatiṣye 'rthavivecana iti | yatnam ārapsye arthavivecane arthavivaraṇe pṛthag-bhāva-karaṇe vā | iyaṁ ca nimittārthā saptamī | arthavivecana-nimittam ity arthaḥ | (OCR 1291–1293)

Tibetan

དོན་དབྱེ་བའི་ཕྱིར་རམ། དོན་དགྲོལ་བའི་ཕྱིར་རམ། སོ་སོར་དབྱེ་བའི་ཕྱིར་བསྒྲིམ་པར་བྱ་བའི་ཕྱིར་ཞེས་བྱ་བ་འདི་ནི་རྒྱུའི་དོན་ཏེ། དོན་རྣམ་པར་དབྱེ་བའི་རྒྱུར་ཞེས་བྱ་བའི་ཐ་ཚིག་གོ། ། (BO_T06_D4032:191a-30)

No change

A clean Pāṇinian-style gloss. The three Skt glosses (vivecane, vivaraṇe, pṛthag-bhāva-karaṇe) map to the three Tib glosses (dbye ba'i phyir, dgrol ba'i phyir, so sor dbye ba'i phyir). The OCR's pṛithagbhāva- with anomalous "ṛi" is the same printing-era convention as mṛiṣā in §2 — normalise to pṛthag-.

Translation

"yatiṣye 'rthavivecane" — "I shall make effort in the discrimination of the meaning, or in [its] explication, or in [its] being set apart". The locative here is a causal locative; the sense is "for the sake of discriminating the meaning".

§9 — kimartham idaṃ śāstraṃ praṇītam: first answer VERIFIED

Yamaguchi text (MS)

kimartham idaṁ śāstraṁ praṇītaṁ(2) buddhānāṁ bhagavatāṁ samyag-nirvikalpa-jñānotpādanārtham | (OCR 1295–1297)

n.2: MS samyag-nirvikalpa; Tib rnam par mi rtog pa'i. Tib LACKS samyag.

One textual variant to note (not emend)

The Tibetan witness lacks the modifier samyag ("correct/right") in the compound; it reads only rnam par mi rtog pa'i ye shes = nirvikalpa-jñāna. This is a real text-historical variant, not a clear error in either direction. Sthiramati's usual idiom does include samyag-nirvikalpa-jñāna (cf. his MAVT chs. 3 and 5), so the Sanskrit reading is plausibly the original and the Tibetan is a slight abridgement. Keep samyag- in the body; note the variant in apparatus.

Translation

"For what purpose was this śāstra composed?" — For the sake of generating the correct, non-discursive cognition of the Buddhas, the Blessed Ones.

§10 — first answer, completion: how the obscurations are eliminated VERIFIED

Yamaguchi text (mixed)

dharma-nairātmya-deśanatayā nirvikalpa-jñānotpādāt tad-abhyāsāc ca niḥśeṣa-savāsana-kleśa-jñeyāvaraṇa-prahāṇaṁ avāpyate | (OCR 1298–1299)

Tibetan

ཆོས་ལ་བདག་མེད་པ་བསྟན་པས་རྣམ་པར་མི་རྟོག་པའི་ཡེ་ཤེས་བསྐྱེད་པ་དང་། དེ་གོམས་པས་བག་ཆགས་དང་བཅས་པའི་ཉོན་མོངས་པ་དང་། ཤེས་བྱའི་སྒྲིབ་པ་མ་ལུས་པར་སྤངས་པ་ཐོབ་པར་འགྱུར་རོ། ། (BO_T06_D4032:191a-37)

No change

Yamaguchi's avāpyate (passive "is attained") at the end matches Tib thob par 'gyur ro. The cluster savāsana-kleśa-jñeyāvaraṇa-prahāṇaṁ is the standard MAVT bookkeeping formula, present in all Sthiramati's works.

Translation

By [their] teaching dharma-nairātmya, non-discursive cognition arises; and by cultivating that, the complete elimination of the kleśa- and the jñeya-obscuration, along with their latencies, is attained.

One concrete proposal: nirātmatā ≈ nāstitā/abhāva

Yamaguchi's reconstruction is structurally correct — two opposing theses about dharma-nairātmya are reported and rejected. But the first thesis as he prints it (sarva-dharma-nirātmatā dharma-nairātmyam = "[dharma-nairātmya =] the [generic] selflessness of all dharmas") fails to capture the Tibetan's pointed first-thesis claim chos thams cad med pa nyid chos la bdag med pa = "[dharma-nairātmya =] the non-existence of all dharmas". The Tibetan med pa nyid renders abhāva or nāstitā, not nirātmatā. The first thesis is the nihilistic Mādhyamika strawman that Sthiramati's school is keen to attack — and "non-existence" is what makes it nihilistic; "selflessness" already concedes the doctrine. Read either:
  • (a) yat sarva-dharma-nāstitā dharma-nairātmyam — "[that thesis according to which] dharma-nairātmya [is] the non-existence of all dharmas".
  • (b) yat sarva-dharma-abhāvo dharma-nairātmyam — same sense, different noun.

The second thesis (antar-vyāpāra-puruṣa-vyatiriktatā "the absence of an internal-active person") matches Tib nang na byed pa'i skyes bu med pa nyid with sufficient precision; keep as printed.

Proposed reading

ayaṃ punar dharma-nairātmyasya virodha-vādo yat sarva-dharma-nāstitā dharma-nairātmyam | antar-vyāpāra-puruṣa-vyatiriktatā ca dharma-nairātmyam — ity atas tat-pratiṣedhena tathābhūta-nairātmya-pratipādanārthaṁ śāstrārambhaḥ |
Yamaguchi
yat sarva-dharma-nirātmatā dharma-nairātmyam
Proposed
yat sarva-dharma-nāstitā dharma-nairātmyam

Translation

Now, [there are also] contradictory views about dharma-nairātmya: [some hold] that the non-existence of all dharmas is dharma-nairātmya; [others hold] that the absence of an internal active person is dharma-nairātmya. The composition of the śāstra is undertaken in order to refute these [views] and to teach nairātmya as it really is.

One textual issue: vineyopāya-dhātv-ātmake vs Tib's missing dhātu

Yamaguchi's reading gives a 5-member compound loka-dhātu / sattva / dharma / vineya / upāya-dhātv-; the Tibetan gives only 5 items but without a final dhātu member: 'jig rten gyi khams (loka-dhātu) + sems can (sattva) + chos (dharma) + 'dul ba (vineya) + thabs kyi bdag nyid (upāya-ātmaka). The trailing dhātv-ātmake in Sanskrit may itself be an intrusion (the compound reads more naturally with just upāya-ātmake). Yamaguchi's apparatus n. 3 already notes this gap. Either keep the Sanskrit and flag the Tibetan as condensed, or emend to upāya-ātmake. The latter is grammatically tidier and matches the canonical Tibetan; I prefer it.
