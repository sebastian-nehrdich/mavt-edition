Sthiramati, Madhyāntavibhāgaṭīkā — Yamaguchi p. 4

OCR lines 1240–1281. Methodology: mavt-reedition-workflow.md. Tibetan witness: BO_T06_D4032:190b-7 → 191a-16.

Page 4 covers Sthiramati's commentary on MAVBh's kārikā 1 phrase sugatātmajaṃ ("the son of the Sugata"). The page starts mid-sentence — see the OCR-gap warning below — and develops: (a) the etymology of sugata (su-gata "well-gone": gone from the kleśa- and jñeya-āvaraṇa to the non-abiding nirvāṇa); (b) a full extension of sugata's qualities (omniscience, basis of all powers, like a wish-fulfilling jewel, effortlessly active for all beings, non-discursive cognition); (c) the sense in which a bodhisattva is "son of the sugata" (sugata-ātmaja) — born from / in the sugata's essence; (d) the celebrated āmalakī simile distinguishing the bodhisattva on the 10th bhūmi (who sees everything as if held in the palm but veiled by fine silk) from the Buddha (whose eye is unveiled); (e) summary of how Maitreya's three "perfections" (avabodha, karuṇā, prajñā) are evidenced in his composing this very śāstra; (f) identification of the vaktṛ as Asaṅga, who received it from Maitreya through empowerment; (g) gloss of the kārikā-particle ca.

OCR-gap warning (between p. 3 and p. 4): The OCR'd file jumps from "etac ca dvayam api … tadvyā-" (end of p. 3) directly to "āvaraṇāc cāpratiṣṭhitaṃ nirvāṇam iti sugataḥ" (start of p. 4 main text), and the page-4 footnote markers (1), (2) attach above the new text. Between these, the corpus-indexed Yamaguchi text (SA_T06_sthmavtyg:23) preserves additional material: tadvyākhyāne vidyate nānyatreti | ata etac chāstram | āha ca | yac chāsti ca kleśaripūn aśeṣān saṃtrāyate durgatito bhavāc ca … — i.e. a śāstra-defining verse plus transition into the sugata gloss. This run-on material is present in Yamaguchi's print but was not OCR'd in the current working file. The two missing pādas of the verse appear separately elsewhere in MAVT (search index). §1 — sugata etymology: āvaraṇāc cāpratiṣṭhitaṃ nirvāṇam iti sugataḥ PROPOSED

Yamaguchi text (begins mid-sentence)

[…savāsana-kleśa-jñeya-]āvaraṇāc cāpratiṣṭhitaṁ nirvāṇam iti sugataḥ | (OCR 1243; footnotes 1, 2)

MS apparatus: n.1 MS āvaraṇā—prati—taṁ, Tib sgrib pa las mi gnas pa'i; n.2 MS …rthaṁ savāsana, Tib de yang bag chags kyi [sgrib pa] thams cad.

Restore the lost left-hand stem

As printed, the sentence begins āvaraṇāc cāpratiṣṭhitaṃ nirvāṇam iti sugataḥ, with the ca-conjunction floating without a first conjunct. The corpus-indexed Yamaguchi text — and apparatus n.2 (Tib de yang bag chags kyi [sgrib pa] thams cad = "all the obscurations with their latencies") — together support a fuller reading suṣṭhu gataḥ savāsana-kleśāvaraṇāj jñeyāvaraṇāc cāpratiṣṭhitaṃ nirvāṇam iti sugataḥ. The two obscurations (kleśāvaraṇa + jñeyāvaraṇa) are exactly what the ca conjoins; their elision is the OCR-gap visible at the page-3/4 transition (see warning above). The opening suṣṭhu gataḥ ("well-gone") is the standard nirukti-style gloss of sugata (cf. Sthiramati's same etymology in his Triṃśikābhāṣya). The compound savāsana-kleśāvaraṇa matches Tib bag chags kyi sgrib pa thams cad.

Proposed reading

suṣṭhu gataḥ savāsana-kleśāvaraṇāj jñeyāvaraṇāc cāpratiṣṭhitaṃ nirvāṇam iti sugataḥ |
Yamaguchi as OCR'd
[…]āvaraṇāc cāpratiṣṭhitaṁ nirvāṇam iti sugataḥ
Proposed (restored)
suṣṭhu gataḥ savāsana-kleśāvaraṇāj jñeyāvaraṇāc cāpratiṣṭhitaṃ nirvāṇam iti sugataḥ

Translation

"Sugata" — i.e., well-gone: gone to the non-abiding nirvāṇa from the kleśa-obscuration (along with its latencies) and from the jñeya-obscuration.

§2 — extended description of the sugata VERIFIED

Yamaguchi text (MS, with two bracketed insertions)

sa ca sarva-vāsanāvaraṇa-prahīṇaḥ sarvathā sarva-dharmāvabodha-svarūpaḥ sarva-vibhūty-āśraya-bhūtaś [cintāma]ṇi-ratnavad acintya-prabhāva-vigrahaḥ sarva-sattvānām anābhogena sarvārtha-[ca]raṇa-samartho nirvikalpaka-jñāna-aviśeṣātmakaḥ [Tib. 20, a] sugataḥ | (OCR 1243–1248)

Tibetan

[implicit — covered by 190b-31 + surrounding segments]; cf. parallel in Pañcaviṃśatisāhasrikāprajñāpāramitā for the wish-fulfilling-jewel image.

One micro-correction: aviśeṣātmakaḥ → viśeṣātmakaḥ

The OCR reads aviśeṣātmakaḥ ("whose essence is non-special"), which contradicts the parallelism: all preceding compounds extol the sugata's special qualities. The TSV machine pass produced nirvikalpakajñānaviśeṣātmakaḥ ("whose essence is the special non-discursive cognition"). The extra a- is almost certainly a typesetting slip (j‌ñāna-viśeṣātmakaḥ printed as jñāna-aviśeṣātmakaḥ through sandhi confusion). Read viśeṣātmakaḥ.

Bracketed reconstructions [cintāma]ṇi and sarvārtha-[ca]raṇa- are well-grounded — cintāmaṇi-ratna ("wish-fulfilling jewel") is a stock Mahāyāna comparand for the Buddha's effortless action, and artha-caraṇa ("course toward the good") is the standard pairing with anābhogena ("effortlessly").

Proposed reading

sa ca sarva-vāsanāvaraṇa-prahīṇaḥ sarvathā sarva-dharmāvabodha-svarūpaḥ sarva-vibhūty-āśraya-bhūtaś cintāmaṇi-ratnavad acintya-prabhāva-vigrahaḥ sarva-sattvānām anābhogena sarvārtha-caraṇa-samartho nirvikalpaka-jñāna-viśeṣātmakaḥ sugataḥ |

Translation

And he is the one in whom all the latency-obscurations are eliminated; whose essence is the all-aspect comprehension of all dharmas; the basis of all sovereign powers; whose body has inconceivable power, like a wish-fulfilling jewel; capable of effortlessly pursuing the good of all sentient beings; whose essence is the unique non-discursive cognition — this is "sugata".

§5 — the āmalakī simile: bodhisattva on the 10th bhūmi PROPOSED

Yamaguchi text

evaṃ sati bodhisattvasya daśamāyāṃ bhūmau pratiṣṭhitasya sarvākāraṃ jñeyaṃ vastu karatalastham ivāmalakī-phalaṃ śuk-āvacchādita-locanasyevābhāsam āyāti |(5) (OCR 1255–1258)

n.5: MS preserves only āmalakaṃ tattvaṃ.

Tibetan

དེ་ལྟ་བས་ན་བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་དཔའ་ས་བཅུ་པ་ལ་རབ་ཏུ་གནས་པ་ནི། ཤེས་བྱའི་དངོས་པོའི་རྣམ་པ་ཐམས་ཅད་སྐྱུ་རུ་ར་ལག་མཐིལ་དུ་གཞག་པ། དར་ལྭ་སྲབ་མོས་གཡོགས་པའི་མིག་ལ་སྣང་བ་བཞིན་ནོ། ། — de lta bas na byang chub sems dpa' sa bcu pa la rab tu gnas pa ni | shes bya'i dngos po'i rnam pa thams cad skyu ru ra lag mthil du gzhag pa | dar lwa srab mos g.yogs pa'i mig la snang ba bzhin no (BO_T06_D4032:190b-31 → 191a-1)

Concrete proposal: śuk-āvacchādita → sūkṣma-paṭ-āvacchādita (or similar)

The compound śuk-āvacchādita-locanasya ("of one whose eye is covered by śuka") has no semantic basis: śuka means "parrot" or "husk", neither of which makes sense here. The Tibetan reads dar lwa srab mos g.yogs pa'i mig la snang ba bzhin no — literally "as appearing to an eye covered by fine silk-cloth" (dar = silk; lwa = cloth; srab mo = fine, thin; g.yogs pa = covered).

Three candidate reconstructions:

  • (a) sūkṣma-paṭ-āvacchādita-locanasya ("of one whose eye is covered by a fine cloth") — directly matches Tib srab mo'i lwa = "fine cloth"; the sūkṣma is identical with srab mo, and the OCR's "śuk-" is plausibly a truncation/corruption of "sūkṣma" if the syllables overlapped in MS shorthand.
  • (b) śukla-paṭ-āvacchādita-locanasya ("of one whose eye is covered by a white cloth") — śukla = "white"; the OCR's "śuk-" matches "śukla-" minus the final liquid. Tib has dar "silk" but does not specify colour, so this reads slightly past the Tibetan.
  • (c) A simple unexplained corruption: read paṭa-āvacchādita-locanasya ("eye covered by a cloth") and discard the corrupt prefix.

Reading (a) is preferable: it accounts both for the OCR trace and for the Tibetan literal sense.

One additional adjustment: locative form

daśamābhūmau as printed has the strange compound daśama-abhūmau (with a-stem locative ending) — almost certainly a typographic mishap for the proper locative daśamāyāṃ bhūmau (a-stem feminine, bhūmi- being feminine) or for the compound daśamabhūmau (loc. sg. masc. of daśama-bhūmi-, treated as masculine here as compound-final). Either is fine; I print daśamāyāṃ bhūmau.

Proposed reading

evaṃ sati bodhisattvasya daśamāyāṃ bhūmau pratiṣṭhitasya sarvākāraṃ jñeyaṃ vastu karatalastham ivāmalakī-phalaṃ sūkṣma-paṭāvacchādita-locanasya ivābhāsam āyāti |
Yamaguchi
… karatalastham ivāmalakī-phalaṃ śuk-āvacchādita-locanasyevābhāsam āyāti
Proposed
… karatalastham ivāmalakī-phalaṃ sūkṣma-paṭāvacchādita-locanasyev ābhāsam āyāti

Translation

Such being the case: for the bodhisattva established on the tenth bhūmi, every aspect of the object of knowledge appears as if [held] in his palm — like an āmalakī fruit — yet [veiled] as if [seen by] an eye covered with fine cloth.

Translation

For here it is shown that, [Maitreya being] born from the very essence of the sugata, [his] perfection of comprehension is exhibited in [the very act of] composing this śāstra; and by [his] composing the śāstra without regard for gain or honor, [his] perfection of compassion and perfection of wisdom are exhibited.

Translation

"vaktāram" means "the maker of the [verbal] exposition"; it construes with the [main verb] "abhyarhya" ("having worshipped"). Others say it construes also with "sugatātmaja". The expounder (vaktṛ) is noble Asaṅga: for it was for him that this śāstra became manifest and was expounded — through the dharma-stream, by the empowerment of noble Maitreya.

§12 — gloss of the particle ca PROPOSED

Yamaguchi text (the lone reconstructed lemma + MS gloss)

cet; samuccaye pādapūraṇe 'dhikavacane vā | anyān api buddhabodhisattvān arcayitvā na kevalaṁ praṇetāraṁ vaktāraṁ [continues p.5] (OCR 1269–1271)

The lemma is ca, not cet

The OCR's "cet;" is plainly a typesetting/OCR slip for ca, the particle being glossed (it is taken from the kārikā vaktāraṃ cāsmad-ādibhyaḥ). The threefold gloss that follows — samuccaye, pādapūraṇe, 'dhikavacane vā ("for conjunction, for pāda-filling, or for emphasis") — is the standard Pāṇinian-style threefold gloss of ca (cf. Cāndravyākaraṇa, Kāśikā, etc.). Tibetan yang zhes bya ba ni bsdu ba'am | rtsa ba bskangs pa'am | lhag pa'i tshig ste (BO_T06_D4032:191a-16) confirms all three glosses, item for item: bsdu ba = samuccaya; rtsa ba bskangs pa = pādapūraṇa; lhag pa'i tshig = adhika-vacana.

Proposed reading

ca — samuccaye pādapūraṇe 'dhika-vacane vā | anyān api buddha-bodhisattvān arcayitvā, na kevalaṁ praṇetāraṁ vaktāraṁ [ca, → p. 5]
Yamaguchi as OCR'd
cet; samuccaye pādapūraṇe 'dhikavacane vā
Proposed
ca — samuccaye pādapūraṇe 'dhika-vacane vā

Translation

"ca" [in the kārikā] — for conjunction, for filling out the pāda, or for emphasis. Other Buddhas and bodhisattvas also having worshipped, not only the composer [and] the expounder [is intended, but… — continues on p. 5]

Lessons for the workflow

  • The OCR has at least one verifiable gap at the p. 3 / p. 4 transition (the śāstra-defining verse yac chāsti ca kleśaripūn … is in the corpus-indexed Yamaguchi text but missing from the OCR file). When a sentence in the OCR starts with a hanging conjunction (ca, tu, vā) and no preceding conjunct is visible, suspect an OCR gap and consult the corpus-indexed Yamaguchi (SA_T06_sthmavtyg) for the missing run-on. This is the one and only situation in which the otherwise-inadmissible Yamaguchi-Sanskrit index is useful as evidence — not for the philology, but for OCR repair.
  • Tibetan ambiguity around dar lwa srab mo. This phrase ("fine silk cloth") in the āmalakī simile likely renders sūkṣma-paṭa, not the OCR's nonsense śuka. Watch for similar OCR-of-MS slips where one of two doubled-letter words gets squashed.
