Sthiramati, Madhyāntavibhāgaṭīkā — Yamaguchi p. 7

OCR lines 1355–1390. Methodology: mavt-reedition-workflow.md. Tibetan witness: BO_T06_D4032:192a-26 → 192b-19.

Page 7 is the central enumeration page of the preface. It finishes the lakṣaṇa-gloss begun at the end of p. 6 (with the earth-element kharalakṣaṇa analogy and the svalakṣaṇa / sāmānyalakṣaṇa distinction); then runs through the remaining five of the seven topics — āvaraṇa (53-fold), tattva (10-fold), pratipakṣa-as-mārga, bhāvanā, avasthā (19-fold, beginning with gotra), phala-prāpti (15-fold), yāna-anuttarya (3-fold) — with brief Pāṇinian-style nirvacana glosses on each. The lower third of the page then explains why this anukrama (order of presentation) was chosen: it conduces to lokottara-jñāna, modelled on the bodhisattva's own training-sequence (adhimukticaryābhūmi → śīla → knowledge of saṁkleśa/vyavadāna → āvaraṇa-jñāna → tattva-jñāna).

The page is mixed: the lemma-gloss pairs are mostly italic (short Pāṇinian formulae), the substantive sentences are MS-attested.

§1–§4 — lakṣaṇa-glosses: kharalakṣaṇa analogy, svalakṣaṇa / sāmānya-lakṣaṇa VERIFIED

Yamaguchi text (MS + italic)

lakṣaṇam [Tib. 21, b] tad yathā pṛthivī-dhātoḥ khara-lakṣaṇo na ca kharatvāt pṛthivī-dhātoḥ pṛthag astīti(1) | atha vā lakṣyate tad iti lakṣaṇam | evaṁ hi saṁkleśo vyavadānaṁ ca saṁkleśa-vyava-dānātmatvena lakṣyata iti lakṣaṇam | atha vā dvayoḥ saṁkleśa-vyavadānayor lakṣaṇaṁ dvividhaṁ svalakṣaṇaṁ ca sāmānya-lakṣaṇaṁ ca | (OCR 1357–1362)

n. 1: MS dhātu…lakṣaṇo na ca…tvāt, Tib khams kyi mtshan nyid sra ba ste = "the earth-element's defining feature is hardness".

Tibetan

དཔེར་ན་སའི་ཁམས་ཀྱི་མཚན་ཉིད་ནི་སྲ་བ་སྟེ། སྲ་བ་ཉིད་ལས་སའི་ཁམས་གུད་ན་མེད་པ་ལྟ་བུའོ། ། ཡང་ན་དེས་མཚོན་པར་བྱ་བས་མཚན་ཉིད་དེ། … ཡང་ན་ཀུན་ནས་ཉོན་མོངས་པ་དང་རྣམ་པར་བྱང་བ་གཉིས་ལས་མཚན་ཉིད་རྣམ་པ་གཉིས་ཏེ། རང་གི་མཚན་ཉིད་དང་སྤྱིའི་མཚན་ཉིད་དོ། ། (BO_T06_D4032:192a-26 → 192a-30)

No change

A clean threefold gloss of lakṣaṇa: (i) defining feature inseparable from substrate (kharatva-of-pṛthivīdhātu analogy); (ii) "that which characterises"; (iii) the saṁkleśa/vyavadāna pair as svalakṣaṇa + sāmānyalakṣaṇa. Tibetan word-by-word: sa'i khams kyi mtshan nyid sra ba = pṛthivī-dhātoḥ khara-lakṣaṇaḥ; rang gi mtshan nyid = svalakṣaṇa; spyi'i mtshan nyid = sāmānya-lakṣaṇa. All verified.

Translation

"lakṣaṇa": just as the defining feature of the earth-element is hardness — and the earth-element is not separate from its hardness — so [too with the seven topics' lakṣaṇa-s]. Or: "lakṣaṇa" is "that which is characterized by it". For thus saṁkleśa and vyavadāna are themselves characterized by being saṁkleśa- and vyavadāna-natured — hence "lakṣaṇa". Or: the lakṣaṇa of the pair (saṁkleśa, vyavadāna) is twofold: svalakṣaṇa and sāmānya-lakṣaṇa.

§5–§6 — āvaraṇa: 53-fold VERIFIED

Yamaguchi text (MS, with bracket inserts)

āvaraṇam iti [idam] āvṛṇoti kuśalān dharmān | vriyante [v]ānena kuśala-dharmā utpatti-vibandhād ity āvaraṇam |(3) tat punas tri-pañcāśat-prakāram | (OCR 1362–1364)

n. 3: MS vriyante 'nena, Tib 'am | 'dis…bsgribs.

Tibetan

སྒྲིབ་པ་ཞེས་བྱ་བ་ནི་དགེ་བའི་ཆོས་རྣམས་ལ་སྒྲིབ་པར་བྱེད་པའམ། འདིས་དགེ་བའི་ཆོས་རྣམས་བསྒྲིབ་པས་སྒྲིབ་པ་སྟེ། སྐྱེ་བ་ལ་བགེགས་བྱེད་པའི་ཕྱིར་སྒྲིབ་པའོ། ། དེ་ཡང་ལྔ་བཅུ་རྩ་གསུམ་མོ། ། (BO_T06_D4032:192b-0)

No change

A neat double nirukti: (a) active "it obstructs the wholesome dharmas" (Tib: dge ba'i chos rnams la sgrib par byed pa); (b) passive "the wholesome dharmas are obstructed by it" (Tib: 'dis dge ba'i chos rnams bsgribs pas). The 53-fold count tat punas tri-pañcāśat-prakāram matches Tib lnga bcu rtsa gsum mo.

Translation

"āvaraṇa": it obstructs the wholesome dharmas; or, the wholesome dharmas are obstructed by it, on account of its blocking [their] arising — hence "obstruction". It is fifty-three-fold.

§7–§8 — tattvam: 10-fold VERIFIED

Yamaguchi text (MS + italic)

tattvam iti tad evedaṁ tat | tasya bhāvas tattvam aviparyāsa ity arthaḥ | tac ca daśa-prakāram | (OCR 1364–1365)

Tibetan

དེ་ཁོ་ན་ཞེས་བྱ་བ་ནི་འདི་ཉིད་དེ་ཡིན་པས་དེའོ། ། (BO_T06_D4032:192b-0)

No change

Standard analytical gloss: "tat eva idam tat" — "this very thing is that"; abstract tattvam = "the state of being-that" = aviparyāsa ("non-error"). The 10-fold count is the standard Yogācāra tattva-enumeration.

Translation

"tattva": that very (tad eva) idam is the tat; its abstract form is tattva, the meaning being "non-error". It is tenfold.

§9–§10 — pratipakṣaḥ = mārga; bhāvanā = tad-abhyāsaḥ VERIFIED

Yamaguchi text (italic + MS)

vipakṣa-prahāṇārthaṁ pakṣaḥ pratipakṣaḥ sa mārgaḥ | tad-abhyāso bhāvanā | (OCR 1366–1367)

No change

The Pāṇinian gloss "vipakṣa-prahāṇa-arthaṁ pakṣaḥ pratipakṣaḥ" ("the pakṣa for the purpose of eliminating the vipakṣa — hence pratipakṣa") is a standard nirvacana. tad-abhyāsaḥ bhāvanā ("cultivation of that is bhāvanā") is again standard.

Translation

"pratipakṣa" — the [right] party (pakṣa) [held] for the purpose of eliminating the contrary party (vipakṣa) — hence pratipakṣa; that is the path. The cultivation of it is the bhāvanā.

§11 — avasthā: 19-fold, beginning with gotra-avasthā VERIFIED

Yamaguchi text (MS)

avasthā tasyāiva saṁtānenotpatti-viśeṣaḥ | sā punar ekānna-viṁśati-prakārā gotrāvasthā-ādikā | (OCR 1367–1368)

No change

avasthā = "a special arising of that very [pratipakṣa-bhāvanā] through the [bodhisattva's] continuum"; 19-fold beginning with gotra-avasthā. This is the standard Yogācāra avasthā-enumeration as found in Bodhisattvabhūmi and elsewhere.

Translation

avasthā is a special arising of that very [pratipakṣa-bhāvanā] through the [bodhisattva's mental] continuum; it is nineteen-fold, beginning with the gotra-stage.

§12 — phala-prāpti: 15-fold, beginning with vipāka-phala VERIFIED

Yamaguchi text (MS + italic)

phalasya lābhaḥ phala-prāptiḥ |(4) tat punaḥ pañcadaśa-prakāraṁ vipāka-phalādi | (OCR 1368–1369)

n. 4: MS prāptes; Tib 'bras bu rnyed pa'o.

One textual note

The MS reading prāpteḥ (gen.) is syntactically awkward — Yamaguchi has implicitly corrected to nominative prāptiḥ, which matches Tib 'bras bu rnyed pa'o ("the obtaining of the fruit"). Accept the correction.

Translation

"phala-prāpti" is the obtaining of the fruit; it is fifteen-fold, beginning with the maturational fruit (vipāka-phala).

§13–§14 — yāna-anuttarya: 3-fold VERIFIED

Yamaguchi text (italic + MS)

yāna-anuttaryam iti — gamyate 'neneti yānam | yānaṁ ca tad ānuttaryaṁ ceti yāna-anuttaryam |(5) tat punas trividhaṁ pratipatty-ānuttarya-ādi | (OCR 1369–1371)

n. 5: MS ānantaryam, Tib bla na med pa.

One real philological correction: MS slip ānantaryam

The MS variant ānantaryam ("immediate succession") is a clear copyist's slip — Tib bla na med pa ("unsurpassed") = Sanskrit anuttarya, not ānantarya. Yamaguchi has corrected this silently. The double-nirvacana gamyate 'neneti yānam ("one goes by this — hence yāna") + yānaṁ ca tad ānuttaryaṁ ca ("a yāna, and that one unsurpassed") is the standard Pāṇinian formulation.

Translation

"yāna-anuttarya": yāna = "one goes by [this]" — hence "vehicle"; and "it is a vehicle and it is unsurpassed" — hence "yāna-anuttarya". It is threefold, beginning with the unsurpassed-ness of [bodhisattva] practice (pratipatty-anuttarya).

§15 — restriction-and-order: saptamo 'rtha iti niyamārtham anukramārthaṁ ca VERIFIED

Yamaguchi text (MS)

saptamo 'rtha iti niyamārtham anukramārthaṁ cāha | etāvanta evārthā nirdiśyante nāto 'nya iti | (OCR 1372–1373)

No change

The bhāṣya's adjective saptamaḥ ("seventh") is glossed as serving two functions: niyama (restriction: "only these many topics") + anukrama (ordering: "in this sequence"). Standard analytic move.

Translation

By [the word] "seventh-topic", [Vasubandhu] indicates both restriction and order: only this many topics are taught, not more.

§16–§17 — why this anukrama: bodhisattva training-sequence VERIFIED

Yamaguchi text (italic + MS)

anukramaḥ punar ayaṁ lokottara-jñāna-anukūlārtham | tathā hi bodhisattvenādhimukti-caryā-bhūmi-sthitena śīla-pratiṣṭhitena prathamaṁ saṁkleśa-vyavadāna-kuśalena bhavitavyam | tataḥ kuśalānāṁ dharmāṇāṁ yasya yad āvaraṇaṁ tasya taj jñeyam | tasyāprahāṇād vimukter asaṁbhavād avijñānaṁ ca prahātuṁ na śakyate doṣādarśanāt | (OCR 1375–1380)

No change

The italic block reconstructs Sthiramati's explanation of why the seven topics are presented in that particular order: the bodhisattva training-sequence (adhimukti-caryā-bhūmi → śīla-pratiṣṭha → saṁkleśa/vyavadāna-knowledge → āvaraṇa-knowledge → tattva-knowledge → mārga-bhāvanā → avasthā → phala → yāna-anuttarya). The reconstruction is grammatically tight and matches the standard Yogācāra path-stage vocabulary. The MS-attested continuation (the logic of why āvaraṇa must come after lakṣaṇa-knowledge) flows naturally from it.

Translation

This order, moreover, is [chosen] for the sake of conducing to supramundane knowledge. Specifically: the bodhisattva, established on the bhūmi of practice-by-confidence (adhimukti-caryā-bhūmi) and rooted in morality, must first become skilled in saṁkleśa and vyavadāna. Then he must come to know which obscuration belongs to which wholesome dharma — for without abandoning [the obscuration] there is no liberation, and ignorance itself cannot be abandoned without seeing its faults.

§19–§20 — tattva = the ālambana whereby the citta is liberated; tatas tenālambanena [→ p. 8] VERIFIED

Yamaguchi text (italic)

tatas tad-āvaraṇād [Tib. 22, a] yenālambanena cittaṁ vimucyate tat tattvaṁ veditavyam | tatas tenālambanena tad [→ p. 8] (OCR 1380–1382)

No change

Defines tattva operationally: the ālambana by which the citta is liberated from the [relevant] āvaraṇa is "that which is to be known as tattva". This is a Sthiramati-flavoured gloss that connects the tattva-topic to the meditative-soteriology framework. Sentence continues onto p. 8.

Translation

Then: the cognitive object (ālambana) by means of which the mind is liberated from that obscuration is to be known as tattva. Then, by that ālambana, that [obscuration]… [→ p. 8].
