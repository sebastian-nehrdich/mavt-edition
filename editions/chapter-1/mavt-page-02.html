OCR lines 1193–1228. Methodology: mavt-reedition-workflow.md. Tibetan witness: BO_T06_D4032:189b-17 → 190a-21. Conventions: cf. page 1.

Page 2 is structurally different from page 1: the manuscript becomes legible mid-page and roughly two-thirds of the text is MS-attested (roman in Yamaguchi). The reconstructed (italic) portions are confined to: the pratīka conclusion iti sarvam; the first sub-thesis tatra praṇetrā vaktum upadiṣṭāt sūtre gauravam utpadyate; the closing of §7 bhrāntam upadiṣṭa iti vṛttyāṃ gauravam utpadyate; the transition idam idānīṃ vaktavyam kīdṛśam śāstrarūpam; and the tail-end dhārmika iti… continuing onto page 3. The MS content develops three theses in series: (a) reverence for the sūtra because its composer is Maitreya; (b) reverence for the vṛtti (commentary) because its composer chain — heard from Asaṅga, written down by Vasubandhu — guarantees fidelity; (c) reverence generated whether the audience follows the person or the dharma.

§2 — first sub-thesis on reverence: tatra praṇetrā vaktum upadiṣṭāt sūtre gauravam utpadyate PROPOSED

Yamaguchi text

tatra praṇetrā vaktum upadiṣṭāt sūtre gāuravam utpadyate | (OCR 1197)

Tibetan

དེ་ལ་མཛད་པས་བཤད་པར་བསྟན་པས་མདོ་ལ་གུས་པ་སྐྱེ་སྟེ། — de la mdzad pas bshad par bstan pas mdo la gus pa skye ste (BO_T06_D4032:189b-17)

Concrete proposal

Yamaguchi reads praṇetrā vaktum upadiṣṭāt — "from [the sūtra's having been] taught by the composer [in order to be] spoken". Two awkward features: (i) the construction upadiṣṭāt + infinitive vaktum at this distance is unusual; (ii) Tibetan mdzad pas bshad par bstan pas analyses as instrumental + accusative-of-purpose + instrumental, not as ablative-of-cause. A cleaner reading that matches Tibetan morphology is tatra praṇetrā vaktum upadiṣṭatvāt sūtre gauravam utpadyate: the abstract upadiṣṭatva- ("the fact of having been taught") takes the ablative -tvāt naturally and renders Tib bstan pas (= upadiṣṭatvāt by causality) idiomatically.

Note the parallel construction Yamaguchi himself prints in the very next reconstructed sub-thesis at §5 below: vaktṛsamādānadvāreṇa vṛttyāṃ gauravam utpadyate — there he uses an instrumental dvāreṇa ("via"). A similar instrumental could be inferred here too: praṇetṛkartṛkavacanāt or praṇetṛ-upadiṣṭatvāt. The TSV machine pass produced tatra praṇetṛvacanāt sūtre gauravam utpadyate ("because of the words of the composer"), which is grammatically the cleanest of all candidates but drops the Tibetan's purpose-clause (bshad par = vaktum / vyākhyātum).

Proposed reading

tatra praṇetrā vaktum upadiṣṭatvāt sūtre gauravam utpadyate |
Yamaguchi
tatra praṇetrā vaktum upadiṣṭāt sūtre gāuravam utpadyate
Proposed
tatra praṇetrā vaktum upadiṣṭatvāt sūtre gauravam utpadyate

Translation

In that case — because [the kārikā-treatise] was taught by [its] composer in order to be expounded, reverence for the sūtra arises.

§4 — Maitreya's bodhisattva qualifications VERIFIED

Yamaguchi text (MS)

sa caikajātipratibaddhāt sarvabodhisattvābhijñādhāraṇīpratisaṁvitsamādhīndriyakṣāntivimokṣaiḥ paramampāraṅgataḥ sarvāsu bodhisattvabhūmiṣu niḥśeṣam api pralīnāvaraṇaḥ | (OCR 1198–1201)

Tibetan

དེ་ཡང་སྐྱེ་བ་གཅིག་གིས་ཐོགས་པའི་ཕྱིར། བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་དཔའི་མངོན་པར་ཤེས་པ་དང་གཟུངས་དང་སོ་སོ་ཡང་དག་པར་རིག་པ་དང་། ཏིང་ངེ་འཛིན་དང་། དབང་དང་བཟོད་པ་དང་། རྣམ་པར་ཐར་པ་ཐམས་ཅད་ཀྱི་དམ་པའི་ཕ་རོལ་ཏུ་སོན་པ། (BO_T06_D4032:189b-20)

One micro-correction: pratibaddhāt → pratibaddhaḥ

The MS reads pratibaddhāt (abl.) — but the syntax requires a nominative agreeing with sa: "He (sa), being bound by only one more birth (ekajātipratibaddhaḥ), having reached the highest perfection in all the bodhisattva powers (pāraṅgataḥ) …, has completely eliminated obscurations (pralīnāvaraṇaḥ) in all bodhisattva stages." The ablative is grammatically unmotivated. Tibetan thogs pa'i phyir ("because [he is] held / bound") would also read more naturally as predicative -pratibaddhaḥ than as ablative -pratibaddhāt. The TSV machine pass produced pratibandhāt (a noun, "because of the binding"), which is syntactically possible but stylistically harsher than the nominative adjective. Read ekajātipratibaddhaḥ.
Yamaguchi prints paramampāraṅgataḥ with the visarga assimilated as a class-1 nasal (paramam-pār). Modernise the sandhi silently to paramaṃ pāraṅgataḥ — the intervocalic-nasal style is just nineteenth-century printing convention. Same with niḥśeṣam api pralīnāvaraṇaḥ (Tib ma lus par bsal ba would also support prahīṇa- in place of pralīna-, but pralīnāvaraṇa is attested elsewhere in Sthiramati's idiolect — keep).

Proposed reading

sa caikajātipratibaddhaḥ sarvabodhisattvābhijñā-dhāraṇī-pratisaṃvit-samādhīndriya-kṣānti-vimokṣaiḥ paramaṃ pāraṃ gataḥ sarvāsu bodhisattvabhūmiṣu niḥśeṣam api pralīnāvaraṇaḥ |

Translation

And he, bound by only one more birth, has reached the highest perfection in all the bodhisattva's superknowledges, retentions, analytical discriminations, concentrations, faculties, forbearances and liberations, and has — without remainder — dissolved [all] obscurations across all the bodhisattva stages.

§6 — Asaṅga and Vasubandhu VERIFIED

Yamaguchi text (MS)

vaktā punar atrācāryaAsaṅgas tasmāc(3) chrutvācāryabhadantaVasubandhus tadbhāṣyam akarot | (OCR 1202–1203)

§7 — fidelity of the bhāṣya: abhrāntam upadiṣṭa iti vṛttyāṃ gauravam utpadyate VERIFIED

Yamaguchi text

tayor apy uttamaprajñāvato 'bhrāntiprativedhadhāraṇopadeśasāmarthyād atra sūtrārtho 'bhrāntam upadiṣṭa iti vṛttyāṃ gauravam utpadyate | (OCR 1203–1205)

Tibetan

[implicit in 190a-3:] མདོའི་དོན་མ་ནོར་བ་བསྟན་ཏོ་ཞེས་འགྲེལ་པ་ལ་གུས་པ་སྐྱེ་སྟེ། — mdo'i don ma nor ba bstan to zhes 'grel pa la gus pa skye ste

One micro-correction: tav apy → tayor apy

OCR reads "tav apy" which is just a printing-era abbreviation for "tayor apy" (genitive dual "of those two", referring to Asaṅga and Vasubandhu). Expand silently. The italic tail abhrāntam upadiṣṭa iti vṛttyāṃ gauravam utpadyate is Yamaguchi's reconstruction of the conclusion of this sentence based on (i) the pattern set by §5 and (ii) Tibetan mdo'i don ma nor ba bstan to zhes 'grel pa la gus pa skye ste ("the sūtra's meaning was taught without error — by this, reverence arises for the vṛtti"). The reconstruction is well-grounded and needs no emendation.

Translation

And — both of [Asaṅga and Vasubandhu] being possessed of the highest wisdom — by virtue of [their] capacity for unerring penetration, retention, and exposition, the sūtra's meaning was here taught without confusion; this is why reverence arises in [the case of] the vṛtti.

One real issue: dharmānusāriṇaḥ requires ye to govern

The Sanskrit as printed reads "and those who are dharmānusārin, of those the understanding of the excellent meaning of sūtra and vṛtti [arises]". This parses, but the Tibetan parallel suggests a participle phrase: ye ca dharme stoṣṭāraḥ ("and those who declare/teach the dharma") = Tib gang yang chos la ston pa ("any who teach [based on] the dharma"). The MS reading dharmānusāriṇaḥ is the usual Yogācāra technical term (cf. śraddhānusārin / dharmānusārin as the two paths in the Madhyāntavibhāgabhāṣya ch. 4). The Tibetan chos la ston pa is loose. The MS reading should stand.

Translation

Likewise: those who take the person [of the composer/expounder] as their authority have, [for them], reverence for the sūtra and the vṛtti arising. And those who are dharma-followers have, for them, an understanding of the excellent meaning of sūtra and vṛtti. And once certainty has arisen, this proves [the doctrine] by way of confidence in the composer and the expounder — not merely by reasoning and scripture; this is why reverence arises in [the case of] the composer and the expounder.

§11 — transition to defining śāstra VERIFIED

Yamaguchi text

idam idānīṃ vaktavyaṃ kīdṛśaṃ śāstrarūpam |(6) (OCR 1209–1210)

MS apparatus n.6: MS preserves only idānīm … rūpam; Tib da ni bstan bcos kyi rang bzhin ci 'dra ba … 'di bshad par bya'o.

Tibetan

ད་ནི་བསྟན་བཅོས་ཀྱི་རང་བཞིན་ཅི་འདྲ་བ་དང་། ཅིའི་ཕྱིར་བསྟན་བཅོས་ཞེས་བྱ་བ་འདི་བཤད་པར་བྱའོ། ། — da ni bstan bcos kyi rang bzhin ci 'dra ba dang | ci'i phyir bstan bcos zhes bya ba 'di bshad par bya'o (BO_T06_D4032:190a-7)

The Tibetan adds a second question

The Tibetan has two questions in series: (a) "what is the nature of a śāstra?" and (b) "why is it called a śāstra?" Yamaguchi's Sanskrit reconstruction collapses these into just (a) — kīdṛśaṃ śāstrarūpam. Restore the second question: idam idānīṃ vaktavyaṃ kīdṛśaṃ śāstrarūpaṃ kasmāc ca śāstram iti | (or kim ca śāstram iti, "and what makes [it] a śāstra"). The extra iti mirrors the pair Tib ci 'dra ba dang ci'i phyir … zhes bya ba. This explains why §13 below opens with śāstraṁ kiṁ ceti — that kiṁ ceti is the answer to the second question, not a stray particle.

Proposed reading

idam idānīṃ vaktavyaṃ kīdṛśaṃ śāstrarūpaṃ kasmāc ca śāstram iti |

Translation

Now this should be said: what is the nature of the śāstra, and on what grounds is it called a "śāstra"?

§12–§14 — definitions of śāstra via vijñapti PROPOSED

Yamaguchi text (MS)

śāstraṃ kiṃ ceti nāmapadavyañjanakāyaprabhāsā vijñaptayaḥ śāstram | atha vā lokottarajñānaprāpakaśabdaviśeṣaprabhāsā vijñaptayaḥ śāstram | kathaṃ vijñaptayaḥ(7) praṇīyanta ucyante vā | praṇetṛvaktṛvijñaptiprabhavatvāt(8) prajñaptīnām nātra doṣaḥ | (OCR 1210–1214)

MS apparatus: n.7 MS …jñaptayā, Tib rnam par rig pa rnams; n.8 Tib mnyan pa'i rnam par rig pa (= śrotavyavijñapti).

Tibetan

མིང་དང་ཚིག་དང་ཡི་གེའི་ཚོགས་སུ་སྣང་བའི་རྣམ་པར་རིག་པ་རྣམས་ནི་བསྟན་བཅོས་སོ། །
ཡང་ན་འཇིག་རྟེན་ལས་འདས་པའི་ཡེ་ཤེས་ཐོབ་པར་བྱེད་པའི་སྒྲའི་ཁྱད་པར་དུ་སྣང་བའི་རྣམ་པར་རིག་པ་རྣམས་བསྟན་བཅོས་སོ། །
རྣམ་པར་རིག་པ་རྣམས་ཇི་ལྟར་མཛད་ཅིང་བརྗོད་ཅེ་ན། མཛད་པ་དང་འཆད་པའི་རྣམ་པར་རིག་པ་ལས་ཉན་པའི་རྣམ་པར་རིག་པ་རྣམས་སྐྱེ་བས་འདི་ལ་ཉེས་པ་མེད་དོ། ། (BO_T06_D4032:190a-9 → 190a-14)

One real correction: prajñaptīnām → vijñaptīnām

The MS preserves jñaptayā (n.7, clearly a fragment of vijñaptayaḥ), and Tibetan throughout this passage has consistently rnam par rig pa = vijñapti. The form prajñaptīnām printed by Yamaguchi at the apparatus-marker location is therefore almost certainly a misreading of vijñaptīnām. The whole sentence is otherwise about vijñapti; there is no doctrinal reason to flip to prajñapti ("designation") mid-sentence. Tibetan de la nyes pa med do ("there is no fault here") confirms the gloss as referring to the vijñapti-chain. Read vijñaptīnām.

Also: the Tibetan supports śrotavya-vijñapti (n.8)

Yamaguchi's apparatus n.8 records the Tibetan reading mnyan pa'i rnam par rig pa = literally śrotavya-vijñapti ("the vijñapti-to-be-heard", i.e. the disciple's hearing cognition). The Tib at 190a-13 confirms: nyan pa'i rnam par rig pa rnams ("the hearing vijñaptis [of the audience] arise from the vijñaptis of the composer and the expounder"). The Sanskrit as printed (vijñaptiprabhavatvāt) implicitly has the same structure but does not make the audience-side śrotavya-/śrotṛ-vijñapti explicit. A more transparent reading would expand: praṇetṛ-vaktṛ-vijñapti-prabhavatvāt śrotṛvijñaptīnāṃ nātra doṣaḥ — "since the [audience's] hearing-vijñaptis arise from the composer's and expounder's vijñaptis, there is no fault here". This makes the sentence-logic explicit and matches both the Tibetan and Yamaguchi's own note 8.

Proposed reading

śāstraṃ kiṃ ceti — nāma-pada-vyañjana-kāya-prabhāsā vijñaptayaḥ śāstram | atha vā lokottara-jñāna-prāpaka-śabda-viśeṣa-prabhāsā vijñaptayaḥ śāstram | kathaṃ vijñaptayaḥ praṇīyante ucyante vā | praṇetṛ-vaktṛ-vijñapti-prabhavatvāt śrotṛvijñaptīnāṃ nātra doṣaḥ |

Translation

And why is it called "śāstra"? — The vijñaptis that appear as the body of names, words, and phonemes are the śāstra. Or: the vijñaptis that appear as a special class of sounds that procure supramundane knowledge are the śāstra. How are these vijñaptis composed, or spoken? Since the [audience's] hearing-vijñaptis arise out of the vijñaptis of the composer and the expounder, no fault attaches here.

Lessons for the workflow

  • OCR catch-word artefacts. Pages 2 & 3 of the OCR file have duplicate / partial words at the page-break (lines 1216–1217) reproducing the catch-word from p. 3 at the bottom of p. 2. Strip these on read; the actual sentence run-on is governed by the page-3 line *sambhārānutpattikarmaṇo nivartate*.
  • Visarga-as-nasal sandhi. The 1934 print uses class-1 nasal sandhi (e.g. paramampāraṅgataḥ) which can be silently modernised to paramaṃ pāraṅgataḥ.
  • p.2 §12 prajñaptīnām for vijñaptīnām is the same class of one-off Yamaguchi slip as p.1 §4's pūjayānānām: a single un-echoed form in a heavily attested contextual vocabulary. The grep test (is this form re-used anywhere else in MAVT?) catches both.
