Page 3 is the shortest page of the preface — eight lines of main text and (in Yamaguchi's print) two short apparatus footnotes attached to it. The OCR of this page is unusually noisy: every line is wrapped in spurious backticks (a Markdown-fence artefact), and the two original footnotes for this page do not appear in the OCR file — they would have been printed on Yamaguchi's p. 3 but were not captured. The full main-text content is also entirely italicised (reconstructed), and the manuscript fragment for the page consists only of praṇeyapravacanāt at line 1 (continuing from the end of p. 2, with an OCR-fragmented compound) and a few syllables flanking the bracketed insertions Yamaguchi marks with [ … ].

Content: the end of §16 from p. 2 (the disciple turns away from non-conducive actions and engages in conducive ones) plus a fresh defining maneuver: śāstra is, alternatively, that which embodies the śāstra-lakṣaṇa — defined as the capacity, in its contemplation and cultivation, to eliminate the afflictions with their latencies and to save [the disciple] from miserable rebirth and [bound] existence. The "these two [features]" of the closing line refers back to kleśa-prahāṇa and durgati/bhava-saṃtāra as the twin marks of a Mahāyāna treatise.

Tibetan

ལུས་དང་ངག་དང་ཡིད་ཚོགས་པར་མི་འབྱུང་བའི་ལས་ལས་བཟློག་པ་དང་། ཚོགས་པར་འབྱུང་བའི་ལས་ལ་འཇུག་པའོ། ། — lus dang ngag dang yid tshogs par mi 'byung ba'i las las bzlog pa dang | tshogs par 'byung ba'i las la 'jug pa'o (190a-17)

One micro-correction (OCR): de-fragment the compound

The OCR's sambhāra-otpatitkar-/-maṇi across the line break is a fragmenting of sambhārotpatti-karmaṇi (sambhāra + utpatti + karmaṇi). The reading is otherwise clean and matches the Tibetan word-for-word: tshogs (=sambhāra) par 'byung ba'i (=-utpatti-) las la 'jug pa'o (=karmaṇi ca pravartate).

Proposed reading

…kāya-vāṅ-manasāṃ sambhārānutpatti-karmaṇo nivartate, sambhārotpatti-karmaṇi ca pravartate |

Translation

[The śāstra] makes [the disciple] turn away from those body-, speech-, and mind- actions that do not give rise to the [bodhisattva] requisites, and engage instead in those actions that do give rise to them.

§2 — second definition of śāstra: śāstra-lakṣaṇasya śāsanāt PROPOSED

Yamaguchi text

atha vā śāstra-lakṣaṇasya śāsanāc chāstram | (OCR 1233)

Tibetan

ཡང་ན་བསྟན་བཅོས་ཀྱི་མཚན་ཉིད་དུ་འཐད་པའི་ཕྱིར་བསྟན་བཅོས་ཏེ། — yang na bstan bcos kyi mtshan nyid du 'thad pa'i phyir bstan bcos te (190a-21)

The Tibetan does not support śāsanāt

Yamaguchi's reading hinges on śāstra-lakṣaṇasya śāsanāt — etymologising śāstra from śās- "to teach": "[it is] a śāstra because it teaches the lakṣaṇa of a śāstra". But the Tibetan reads mtshan nyid du 'thad pa'i phyir = "because [it] accords with the lakṣaṇa [of a śāstra]" ('thad pa = yujyate / upapadyate / saṃbhavati, not śāsti). The construction is "[it is called] a śāstra because the śāstra-characteristic is applicable" — a definition-by-fit rather than a definition-by-teaching.

Two candidate Sanskrit readings, both plausible:

  • (a) atha vā śāstra-lakṣaṇa-saṃbhavāc chāstram — "Or it is a śāstra because the śāstra-lakṣaṇa is present in it"
  • (b) atha vā śāstra-lakṣaṇa-yogāc chāstram — "Or it is a śāstra because it is connected with the śāstra-lakṣaṇa"

Both render Tib 'thad pa'i phyir naturally. (a) is slightly preferable as it picks up the subsequent gloss tac ca śāstra-lakṣaṇaṃ yat upadeśo… ("and that śāstra-lakṣaṇa is…") which sets up a specifying relative — better governed by an existential than by a yoga-relation.

Proposed reading

atha vā śāstra-lakṣaṇa-saṃbhavāc chāstram |
Yamaguchi
atha vā śāstra-lakṣaṇasya śāsanāc chāstram
Proposed
atha vā śāstra-lakṣaṇa-saṃbhavāc chāstram

Translation

Or alternatively: [the text is] a śāstra because the characteristic of a śāstra is found [in it].

§3 — defining the śāstra-lakṣaṇa: tac ca śāstra-lakṣaṇaṃ yad… PROPOSED

Yamaguchi text

[tac ca śā]stra-lakṣaṇam(1) yad upadeśo bhāsamāno [’bhyastaḥ] savāsanākleśaprahāṇāya(2) [padyate] nirantaradīrghavidhatva-duḥkha-bhītāyāś ca durgater bhavāc ca saṃtrāyate | (OCR 1234–1236)

Yamaguchi's bracketed material ([tac ca śā], [’bhyastaḥ], [padyate]) is his own insertion to complete fragmentary MS. The footnote markers (1) and (2) refer to apparatus notes that were not OCR-captured for page 3.

Tibetan

ལུང་མནོས་པ་གོམས་པར་བྱས་པས་བག་ཆགས་དང་བཅས་པའི་ཉོན་མོངས་པ་སྤོང་བར་འགྱུར་བ་དང་། བར་ཆད་མེད་པ་ཡུན་རིང་བའི་སྡུག་བསྔལ་དྲག་པོ་སྣ་ཚོགས་ཀྱིས་འཇིགས་པའི་ངན་སོང་རྣམས་དང་། སྲིད་པ་ལས་སྐྱོབ་པ་གང་ཡིན་པ་དེ་ནི་བསྟན་བཅོས་ཀྱི་མཚན་ཉིད་དོ། ། — lung mnos pa goms par byas pas bag chags dang bcas pa'i nyon mongs pa spong bar 'gyur ba dang | bar chad med pa yun ring ba'i sdug bsngal drag po sna tshogs kyis 'jigs pa'i ngan song rnams dang | srid pa las skyob pa gang yin pa de ni bstan bcos kyi mtshan nyid do (190a-17 → 190a-24)

Three concrete proposals

(a) bhāsamāno → bhāvyamāno. The Tibetan has lung mnos pa goms par byas pas = "by having heard (mnos pa = śruta) [and] cultivated (goms par byas pa = bhāvita / abhyasta) the teaching". Yamaguchi's bhāsamāno ("shining / being illumined") does not render either of these verbs. The far more natural back-translation is bhāvyamāno ("being cultivated" / "being meditated upon"), which lines up exactly with Tib goms par byas pas. The Devanagari bhā‌yamāno / bhāvyamāno / bhāsamāno are easily confusable in MS shorthand. Yamaguchi's bracketed insertion [’bhyastaḥ] independently confirms that he wanted the "cultivation" sense to be present — read bhāvyamāno 'bhyastaḥ, where the doublet (passive present + past participle) parallels Tib mnos pa goms par byas pas (heard + cultivated).

(b) vidhatva → vividha-tīvra. The OCR's vidhatva in nirantara-dīrgha-vidhatva- is a corruption of vividha-tīvra- ("various, intense"). Tib drag po sna tshogs = "various intense (sufferings)"; drag po = tīvra, sna tshogs = vividha. Yamaguchi knew this — his apparatus n. 2 (not in OCR) presumably noted the MS reading — and his TSV-machine-pass clone reproduces the same garble. Read nirantara-dīrgha-vividha-tīvra-duḥkha-bhīta-.

(c) Re-case the predicate: -bhītāyāś → -bhītān. Yamaguchi's duḥkha-bhītāyāś (gen./dat./abl. sg. fem.) is grammatically anomalous — it modifies neither durgater (gen. sg. fem., which is the source of peril) nor any clear feminine antecedent. The Tibetan structure is sdug bsngal … 'jigs pa'i ngan song … skyob pa — "[the śāstra] saves [the disciples], frightened by suffering, from miserable rebirth and existence". That requires a predicative nom./acc. of the implicit object (the disciples) qualified by -bhīta-. The cleanest reading is -duḥkha-bhītān (acc. pl. m., agreeing with an unstated object). Alternatively -duḥkha-bhītānāṃ (gen. pl. m., subject-of-protection construction with saṃtrāyate). Either fixes the case-clash.

Proposed reading

tac ca śāstra-lakṣaṇaṃ yad upadeśo bhāvyamāno 'bhyastaḥ savāsana-kleśa-prahāṇāya padyate, nirantara-dīrgha-vividha-tīvra-duḥkha-bhītān ca durgater bhavāc ca saṃtrāyate |
Yamaguchi
tac ca śāstra-lakṣaṇam yad upadeśo bhāsamāno ['bhyastaḥ] savāsanakleśaprahāṇāya [padyate] nirantaradīrghavidhatva-duḥkhabhītāyāś ca durgater bhavāc ca saṃtrāyate
Proposed
tac ca śāstra-lakṣaṇaṃ yad upadeśo bhāvyamāno 'bhyastaḥ savāsana-kleśa-prahāṇāya padyate, nirantara-dīrgha-vividha-tīvra-duḥkha-bhītān ca durgater bhavāc ca saṃtrāyate

Translation

And the characteristic of a śāstra is this: that the teaching, [when] meditated upon and cultivated, conduces to the elimination of the afflictions together with their latencies; and that it saves [the disciples], who are terrified by the continuous, long-lasting, various, intense sufferings, from miserable rebirth and from [bound] existence.

§4 — summing up: tasmāt kleśa-ripu-śāsanāt …śāstra-lakṣaṇam VERIFIED

Yamaguchi text

tasmāt kleśa-ripu-śāsanāt bhava-durgati-saṃtārāc ca śāstra-lakṣaṇam | (OCR 1236–1237)

Tibetan

དེའི་ཕྱིར་ཉོན་མོངས་པའི་དགྲ་འཆོས་པ་དང་། ངན་འགྲོ་དང་སྲིད་པ་ལས་སྐྱོབ་པས་བསྟན་བཅོས་ཀྱི་མཚན་ཉིད་དེ། — de'i phyir nyon mongs pa'i dgra 'chos pa dang | ngan 'gro dang srid pa las skyob pas bstan bcos kyi mtshan nyid de (190a-24)

No change

Tibetan 'chos pa ("to discipline, correct") supports śāsana in its punitive sense ("disciplining the foe-of-afflictions"). The compound kleśa-ripu-śāsanāt is a well-attested Sthiramati-style abstract genitive. The protection-formula bhava-durgati-saṃtārāc ca matches Tib ngan 'gro dang srid pa las skyob pas word for word.

Translation

Therefore, by reason of [its] disciplining the foe that is the afflictions, and [its] rescuing [the disciple] from miserable rebirth and [bound] existence, [this is] the characteristic of a śāstra.

§5 — these two features are unique to Mahāyāna: etac ca dvayam api… VERIFIED

Yamaguchi text (continues onto page 4)

etac ca dvayam api sarvasmin mahāyāne sarvasmiṃś ca tadvyā[khyāne vidyate nānyatreti | ata etac chāstram |] (OCR 1237–1238 → continues p. 4)

Tibetan

དེ་གཉིས་ཀྱང་ཐེག་པ་ཆེན་པོ་ཐམས་ཅད་དང་། དེ་བཤད་པ་ཐམས་ཅད་ལ་ཡོད་ཀྱི་གཞན་ལ་མེད་དེ་དེའི་ཕྱིར་འདི་ནི་བསྟན་བཅོས་སོ། ། — de gnyis kyang theg pa chen po thams cad dang | de bshad pa thams cad la yod kyi gzhan la med de de'i phyir 'di ni bstan bcos so (190a-27)

No change

The Sanskrit reads transparently: "And these two [features] are found in all the Mahāyāna and in every explanation of it [tadvyākhyāne], not elsewhere — therefore this is a śāstra." The Tib de bshad pa thams cad la renders sarvasmiṃś ca tadvyākhyāne with vyākhyāna (bshad pa); the closing 'di ni bstan bcos so matches Yamaguchi's reconstructed ata etac chāstram.

Translation

And both of these [features] are found in all Mahāyāna [scriptures] and in every commentary on the Mahāyāna — not elsewhere; therefore this [text] is a śāstra.
