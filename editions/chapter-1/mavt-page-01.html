The MAVT Sanskrit currently in the search index (SA_T06_sthmavt / SA_T06_sthmavtyg) is identical to the text we are trying to fix and was treated as inadmissible evidence throughout.

Page 1 is unusually difficult because Yamaguchi printed almost the entire page in italics — i.e. as conjectural reconstruction. The MS for this folio is fragmentary beyond the few words his apparatus quotes. The proposals below therefore lean heavily on the Tibetan (which IS extant and reliable) and on internal grammar.

What page 1 contains

Sthiramati's preface to the entire ṭīkā. After a brief homage to Mañjuśrī, he frames Vasubandhu's bhāṣya opening: noble people, before acting, worship their teacher and tutelary deity; Vasubandhu, who is such a noble person, therefore opens his bhāṣya with the homage verse śāstrasyāsya praṇetāram abhyarhya sugatātmajaṃ / vaktāraṃ cāsmadādibhyo yatiṣye 'rthavivecane // — and the rest of page 1 glosses why that homage was offered (it yields merit; it generates reverence among the audience for the sūtra, its composer, its expounder, and its commentary). The page ends with the second of two parallel atha vā framings.

Six sentence-units: §1 maṅgala (Sthiramati's own), §2 a long introductory sentence framing Vasubandhu's homage, §3 the rhetorical "what merit is obtained?", §4 the answer (merit accumulates), §5 the consequence (the work is completed without obstacle), §6 the parallel atha vā framing.

Concrete proposals

(a) Restore the MS singular subject and verb. Yamaguchi's uttamajanā hi …pravartanta (plural) is his own correction; the MS, as he himself prints in n. 1, has the singular middle pravartata. The Tibetan ya rabs dag ni phal cher … 'jug ste is not decisive between singular and plural: the particle dag after a generic noun often marks a class rather than a strict plural, and the verb 'jug ste is unmarked for number. Sanskrit śāstra-openings of this generic type are routinely cast in the third singular (uttamajano hi prāyaśo…). The cleaner, MS-preserving reading is therefore singular throughout.

(b) Drop the inserted bhāṣyam; rebuild the governance of cikīrṣus. The MS preserves cikīrṣus tat (n. 3); the word it governs is lost. Yamaguchi filled the gap with Madhyāntavibhāga-sūtra-bhāṣyam, giving "wishing to compose the MAV-sūtra-bhāṣya". This is questionable on three counts:

  • The Tibetan says mdo rnam par bshad par 'dod pas — "by [me] wishing to vyākhyātum / vivecayitum the sūtra", with no equivalent of bhāṣyam. The Tibetan word rnam par bshad pa can render either vyākhyāna or bhāṣya, but the structural slot here is governed by 'dod pas (= icchatā / cikīrṣatā) and the object slot has no separate noun glossing bhāṣyam.
  • Yamaguchi's own Japanese translation of the same passage glosses the phrase as 中邊分別經を釋せんと願 — "wishing to explain the MAV-sūtra", not "wishing to compose the bhāṣya". His Sanskrit and his Japanese do not agree.
  • Sthiramati is not composing the bhāṣya — Vasubandhu did. Sthiramati is composing a ṭīkā on the bhāṣya, which "explicates" (vyākhyā) the sūtra. The verbal frame should match.

(c) The uttamajananayam anuvartī formation follows Yamaguchi's reading of the Tibetan ya rabs kyi tshul + his decision to make it a present participle. Tibetan tshul-can renders the possessive suffix -vat; the chosen -anuvartin ("following after") is permissible but not the most literal reverse-gloss. uttamajananayavān would map the Tibetan more literally. We retain Yamaguchi's reading here and note the alternative.

Independent Sanskrit parallels

Nyāyakalikā (Jayanta Bhaṭṭa) — same śāstra-opening topos: śāstrārambhe praṇamato devatāṃ śāstrasya praṇetāraṃ guruṃ ca stautīkṣasya pūrvoddeśaṃ namasyati … SA_GSP29_nyaka:360. Independent confirmation that devatāṃ guruṃ ca praṇam-/abhyarc- at the head of a treatise is a stock topos; Sthiramati's wording belongs to the same family but does not match it verbatim. No verbatim Sanskrit parallel for uttamajananayam anuvartī surfaces in the corpus outside MAVT itself — it is plausibly Sthiramati's own coinage.

Proposed reading

āryamañjuśriye kumārabhūtāya namaḥ ||
uttamajano hi prāyaśo guruṃ śraddhādevatāṃ cābhyarcya karmasu pravartata ity ayam apy aham uttamajananayam anuvartī madhyāntavibhāgasūtravyākhyānaṃ cikīrṣus tatpraṇetur vaktuś ca pūjāṃ kṛtvā tadarthavibhāgāya prayukta iti pratipādayann āha — śāstrasyāsya praṇetāram ityādi |

Justification

(a) uttamajano … pravartata: MS-preserved verb (Yamaguchi n. 1) restored, with matching singular subject; Tibetan does not oppose. (b) sūtravyākhyānaṃ cikīrṣus tat: Tibetan mdo rnam par bshad par 'dod pas + the MS-preserved fragment cikīrṣus tat can be reconstructed as a single tatpuruṣa compound governed by cikīrṣur, with tat resuming as the genitive antecedent of the next clause's tat-praṇetur. This (i) preserves both MS-attested words intact, (ii) matches the Tibetan verb (vyākhyātum / vyākhyāna, not bhāṣyam), and (iii) agrees with Yamaguchi's own Japanese construal. (c) The bracketed [iti jñāpanārthaṁ] remains — it is supported by Tibetan zhes shes par bya ba'i phyir; Yamaguchi's bracketing should be retained.

§4 — guṇavato hitakāriṇaś ca pūjayatām eṣāṃ puṇyam upacīyate PROPOSED

Yamaguchi text

guṇavadbhyo hitakāribhyaś ca pūjayānānāṁ(4) eṣāṁ puṇyam upacīyate | (p. 1, lines 6–7; OCR lines 1173–1175)

MS apparatus (note 4): MS has only the trailing …yeṣām. No Tibetan equivalent supplied by Yamaguchi at this footnote.

Tibetan

ཡོན་ཏན་དང་ལྡན་པ་དང་། ཕན་འདོགས་འདོགས་པ་ལ་མཆོད་ན་བསོད་ནམས་འཕེལ་བར་འགྱུར་རོ། །
yon tan dang ldan pa dang | phan 'dogs 'dogs pa la mchod na bsod nams 'phel bar 'gyur ro
(BO_T06_D4032:189b-9)

Concrete proposals

(a) Drop the datives. Yamaguchi prints the worshipped persons in the dative (guṇavadbhyo hitakāribhyaś ca). Sanskrit pūjayati takes its object in the accusative, not the dative. The Tibetan la is ambiguous between dat./loc./acc., so it does not force the dative. Read instead guṇavato hitakāriṇaś ca (acc. pl., m.).

(b) Replace the unidiomatic middle participle. pūjayānānām is a present middle participle in the genitive plural. The middle of √pūj is rare and not idiomatic of the "worshipping" sense (which is normally active). The MS-fragment …yeṣām can equally well represent the active participle pūjayatām (gen. pl. of pūjayat-) — the final -tām eṣām sequence, mangled in damage, is at least as plausible as -nānām eṣām. Active pūjayatām is the register-appropriate form in commentarial Sanskrit.

(c) Resulting reading: guṇavato hitakāriṇaś ca pūjayatām eṣāṃ puṇyam upacīyate — "of these people who worship the virtuous and the benefactors, merit accumulates." This is also the reading independently produced by an earlier machine pass logged in references/T06sthmavt-yg-trans.tsv §2 (the TSV is treated here as orientation, not evidence, but the convergence is suggestive).

Internal MAVT check

A grep across the entire MAVT OCR (mavt_yamaguchi_ocr.txt) for the form pūjayānānām returns only this one occurrence (OCR line 1175). The form is not echoed anywhere else in Sthiramati's own surviving Sanskrit. By contrast, present active participles of pūj- (with normal active morphology) are common in the Yogācāra commentarial corpus. The absence of corroboration is evidence — Yamaguchi's pūjayānānām was a reconstruction of desperation, not a confirmed reading.

Concrete proposals

(a) Read samārambhe (loc.) for samārambhād (abl.). The Tibetan yang dag par rtsom pa la uses the la particle, which renders Skt locative far more naturally than ablative. Yamaguchi's ablative is a stylistic preference; the Tibetan and the parallelism with the preceding puṇya upacite (loc. absolute) both point to samārambhe. Best as a continuation of the locative absolute: puṇya upacite samārambhe… — "when merit has been accumulated [and] when the undertaking is in train…".

(b) Read anupahatāḥ (nom. pl.) for anupahatān (acc. pl.). The participle agrees with the (implicit) plural subject of samāpayanti — i.e. the worshippers of §4 (eṣām). An accusative makes no sense syntactically; this is almost certainly an OCR or typesetting slip, not a deliberate reading. The Tibetan mi tshugs par (adverbial, "unobstructedly") is consistent with either case but favours a predicative nominative.

(c) The verb form samāpayanti ("they complete [it]") is supportable: the Tib rdzogs par 'gyur ro is impersonal-passive in form but can render an active Sanskrit verb whose subject is understood. Yamaguchi's reading stands.

Proposed reading

puṇya upacite samārambhe vighnavināyakair anupahatā alpena prayāsena samāpayantīti |

Justification

The two changes (loc. samārambhe; nom. anupahatāḥ) are minor but tidy up grammatical irregularities Yamaguchi himself flagged in his own apparatus (n. 5 reproduces the MS fragment ending in …nārambha, which is just as compatible with samārambhe as with samārambhāt). No independent Sanskrit parallel to this exact sentence surfaces in the /primary/ search; the formula is plausibly Sthiramati's own. An internal grep over MAVT for samārambh- in any case returns no second occurrence — the page is a sole witness, so the case must be decided on Tibetan and grammar alone.

The difficulty

The Tibetan here is itself dense and admits more than one Sanskrit back-construal. The MS preserves only praṇeyapravacanāt — two words — and the rest is open. Yamaguchi's compound praṇetṛ-praṇeya-[vaktṛ-vākya-samādāna-]pravacanāt tries to unify the whole Tibetan in a single compound, with a square-bracketed insertion (vaktṛvākyasamādāna-) to absorb the Tibetan yang dag par byin par bsnyad pas. The result is awkward — both gerundive (praṇeya "to be composed") and the parallel asymmetry between agent-compounds (praṇetṛ, vaktṛ) and verbal-noun compounds (vākyasamādāna, pravacana) are unusual.

Concrete improvement (one change defensible)

Read praṇīta- for praṇeya-. The Tibetan mdzad pas gsungs pa is past-passive ("what was composed by the composer"), not gerundive ("what is to be composed"). The MS-preserved praṇeya is itself reconstructed by Yamaguchi from the trailing °eyapravacanāt; the same letters can read praṇīta-pravacanāt if the preceding word ended in -i + ta becoming -iyat (read as -eya). The past passive is also better Sanskrit for what is clearly being described.

The rest of the compound — Yamaguchi's bracketed vaktṛ-vākya-samādāna- — remains genuinely contested. Two alternatives, neither decisively better:

  • praṇetrā praṇītasya vaktrā vyākhyātasya ca samyag-dāna-pravacanāt — "from the proper imparting and discoursing of what was composed by the composer and what was expounded by the expounder" (matches the Tibetan word order more literally; sacrifices Yamaguchi's single-compound elegance).
  • praṇetṛ-praṇīta-vaktṛ-vyākhyāta-samyag-grahaṇa-pravacanāt — "from the proper reception and expounding of what was composed by the composer and what was expounded by the expounder" (preserves Yamaguchi's single-compound move; renders Tib byin par bsnyad pas as grahaṇa-pravacana).

Proposed reading (minimal)

atha vā praṇetṛ-praṇīta-[vaktṛ-vākya-samādāna-]pravacanāt sūtra-praṇetṛ-vaktṛ-vṛttiṣu gauravotpādanārtham āha — śāstrasyāsya praṇetāram iti sarvam |

Justification

The one change argued here (praṇeya → praṇīta) is narrow and well-supported: the Tibetan past-participial gsungs pa demands a past participle, not a gerundive, and the MS-fragment is equally compatible with either. The remainder of the compound is marked as non liquet: the Tibetan admits multiple Sanskrit back-readings and the MS preserves too little for a confident reconstruction. A human editor should choose between the two alternatives offered above; the agent has no philological reason to prefer one over the other.
