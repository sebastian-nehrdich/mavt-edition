Sthiramati, Madhyāntavibhāgaṭīkā — Yamaguchi p. 9

OCR lines 1425–1437. Methodology: mavt-reedition-workflow.md. Tibetan witness: BO_T06_D4032:193b-4.

Page 9 is — like page 3 — a very short, entirely italicised page (8 lines of main text, one footnote). It contains the third alternative explanation of the saptārtha-order: confusion (sammoha) about sad- and asad-dharmas in a sentient being (perhaps: in a bodhisattva — see n. 1) is removed by lakṣaṇa-nirdeśa; once the confusion is removed, āvaraṇa-skill comes, then tattva (since tattva is what is obscured by the āvaraṇa), then pratipakṣa-bhāvanā (since penetration-and- cultivation of tattva eliminates the āvaraṇa), then avasthā (to acquire skill in the divisions of bhāvanā), then phala (which is produced by the avasthā), and finally yāna-anuttarya (since all of this is reached only via the Mahāyāna).

This is the cleanest of the three alternative anukrama-rationales and reads as a self-contained argumentative chain. Yamaguchi's reconstruction is well-formed throughout.

§1 — alt. view 3, opening: sattvasya sad-asad-dharma-sammohasya … NON LIQUET

Yamaguchi text (all reconstructed)

(1)anyac ca sattvasya sad-asad-dharma-sammohasya apavāda-samāropa-prahāṇārthaṁ lakṣaṇa-nirdeśaḥ | (OCR 1427–1429)

n. 1: Tib reads sems pa; "but sems pa would be sems dpa'" — i.e. Yamaguchi notes that the Tibetan sems pa (thinking/sentient) should perhaps be read as sems dpa' (bodhisattva).

Tibetan

གཞན་ཡང་སེམས་པ་ཡོད་པ་དང་མེད་པའི་ཆོས་ལ་རྨོངས་ནས། སྐུར་པ་འདེབས་པ་དང་། སྒྲོ་འདོགས་པ་སྤང་བའི་ཕྱིར་མཚན་ཉིད་བཤད་དོ། ། — gzhan yang sems pa yod pa dang med pa'i chos la rmongs nas | skur pa 'debs pa dang | sgro 'dogs pa spang ba'i phyir mtshan nyid bshad do

The sems pa / sems dpa' question

Yamaguchi reads the Tibetan sems pa ("thinking"/"sentient") as Skt sattva ("sentient being"). His n. 1 records that the Tibetan sems pa may itself be a corruption for sems dpa' (bodhisattva), which would make the gen. read bodhisattvasya rather than sattvasya.
  • (a) sattvasya (Yamaguchi's printed reading): "for [removing] the apavāda and samāropa of the sentient being's confusion about sad- and asad-dharmas". sems pa in the Tibetan can render sattva, cetas, or cintā — the first is most plausible here.
  • (b) bodhisattvasya (Yamaguchi's alternative): better fits the doctrinal frame (the addressee throughout MAVT is the bodhisattva), but requires emending the Tibetan sems pa → sems dpa', which is an intervention into the canonical Tibetan witness.

Either is defensible. The Sanskrit sattvasya reads slightly more naturally — the genitive can be construed as "the kind of sammoha that a sentient being [generally] has", a general not specific designation. Keep Yamaguchi's sattvasya in the body; note (b) in apparatus.

Proposed reading

anyac ca — sattvasya sad-asad-dharma-sammohasya apavāda-samāropa-prahāṇārthaṁ lakṣaṇa-nirdeśaḥ |

Translation

Yet again: in order to eliminate the apavāda (denial) and samāropa (superimposition) of the sentient being's confusion about sad- and asad-dharmas, [Maitreya gives] the lakṣaṇa-teaching [first].

§2 — prahīṇa-sammohasya āvaraṇa-kauśalārtham āvaraṇam VERIFIED

Yamaguchi text (italic)

prahīṇa-sammohasya āvaraṇa-kauśalārtham āvaraṇam | (OCR 1429–1430)

Tibetan

རྨོངས་པ་སྤངས་ནས་སྒྲིབ་པ་ལ་མཁས་པར་བྱ་བའི་ཕྱིར་སྒྲིབ་པའོ། ། — rmongs pa spangs nas sgrib pa la mkhas par bya ba'i phyir sgrib pa'o

No change

Direct Tib correspondence: rmongs pa spangs nas = prahīṇa-sammohasya (loose gen.-of-state); sgrib pa la mkhas par bya ba'i phyir = āvaraṇa-kauśalārtham; sgrib pa'o = āvaraṇam. Each Skt word maps onto one Tib word.

Translation

For [the one] whose confusion has been eliminated, [the topic] āvaraṇa [comes next], for the sake of [acquiring] skill in [recognising] the obscuration.

§3 — tenāvṛtaṁ tattvam iti tattva-kauśalārthaṁ tadanantaraṁ tattvam VERIFIED

Yamaguchi text (italic)

tenāvṛtaṁ tattvam iti tattva-kauśalārthaṁ tadanantaraṁ tattvam | (OCR 1430–1431)

Tibetan

དེས་དེ་ཁོ་ན་བསྒྲིབས་པས་དེ་ཁོ་ན་ལ་མཁས་པར་བྱ་བའི་ཕྱིར་དེའི་འོག་ཏུ་དེ་ཁོ་ནའོ། ། — des de kho na bsgribs pas de kho na la mkhas par bya ba'i phyir de'i 'og tu de kho na'o

No change

Verbatim Tib match: des = tena ("by that [āvaraṇa]"); de kho na bsgribs pa = tenāvṛtaṁ tattvam; de kho na la mkhas par bya ba'i phyir = tattva-kauśalārthaṁ; de'i 'og tu de kho na'o = tadanantaraṁ tattvam. Reconstruction is tight.

Translation

Since it is by that [obscuration] that the truth is obscured, in order to [acquire] skill in [recognising] truth, immediately after [comes] tattva.

§4 — tattva-prativedha-bhāvanayā āvaraṇa-prahāṇaṁ … tattvānantaraṁ pratipakṣa-bhāvanā VERIFIED

Yamaguchi text (italic)

tattva-prativedha-bhāvanayā āvaraṇa-prahāṇaṁ bhavatīti tattva-anantaraṁ pratipakṣa-bhāvanā | (OCR 1431–1432)

No change

Standard Sthiramati formulation: the compound tattva-prativedha-bhāvanā ("the cultivation of the penetration of truth") yields āvaraṇa-prahāṇa, hence the order tattva → pratipakṣa-bhāvanā. Tib (193b-4 et seq., implicit): the chain is preserved in the page's overall argumentative structure.

Translation

Since the elimination of the obscuration comes about through the cultivation of penetrating [that] truth, immediately after tattva [comes] pratipakṣa-bhāvanā.

§5 — tat-prabheda-kauśalārtham avasthā … avasthā-anantaraṁ phalam VERIFIED

Yamaguchi text (italic)

tat-prabheda-kauśalārtham avasthā | avasthayā phalaṁ prabhāvitam ity avasthā-anantaraṁ phalaṁ tat-kauśalotpādanārtham | (OCR 1433–1434)

No change

Continues the chain: avasthā as "skill in the divisions [of bhāvanā]"; phala as "produced by the avasthā"; the trailing tat-kauśalotpādanārtham ("for the sake of producing skill in that [phala]") gives the rationale for introducing the phala as a separate item.

Translation

For [acquiring] skill in the divisions [of bhāvanā, comes] avasthā. And since the fruit is produced by the avasthā, immediately after avasthā [comes] phala, for the sake of producing skill in [that fruit].

§6 — closing: sarvam etan mahāyānam āgamya iti yāna-anuttaryam ante nirdiṣṭam VERIFIED

Yamaguchi text (italic)

sarvam etan mahāyānam āgamyeti yāna-anuttaryam ante nirdiṣṭam [iti] | (OCR 1434–1435)

No change

The verbal form āgamya iti ("having reached … so") is good Sthiramati Sanskrit; the closing ante nirdiṣṭam ("taught at the end") rounds off the explanation of why yāna-anuttarya is the seventh and final topic. The bracketed [iti] is Yamaguchi's standard closing punctuation-marker.

Translation

All this is approached by way of the Mahāyāna — so [the topic] yāna-anuttarya is set forth at the end.
