Introduction

Chapter 1 of Sthiramati's Madhyāntavibhāgaṭīkā is the Lakṣaṇapariccheda. It opens with kārikā I.1, the celebrated abhūtaparikalpa-asti / dvayam tatra na vidyate / śūnyatā vidyate tv atra / tasyām api sa vidyate, and unfolds the basic Yogācāra metaphysics: abhūta-parikalpa (= the psychophysical stream of cognition) exists, the duality of grāhya/grāhaka does not, but emptiness does — and within that emptiness, the abhūta-parikalpa is again found. Sthiramati's commentary on this opening is dense and contains long italicised (reconstructed) stretches where the Nepalese MS fails. The walk-through that follows covers Yamaguchi pp. 10–14.

The Vyākaraṇa-paribhāṣā maxim

The maxim yathā-uddeśas tathā nirdeśaḥ ("the exposition follows the order of the outline") is a classical Sanskrit śāstric organising principle of Vyākaraṇa pedigree — first home in Patañjali's Mahābhāṣya, later codified in the paribhāṣā literature (Paribhāṣenduśekhara). Sthiramati's invocation of it here is one of several signs of his cross-śāstric training.

Patañjali, Mahābhāṣya — SA_GS24_pmbhasuu:23192 (also at :18311, :16496, :7925, :4680, :27944). Vyākaraṇa locus classicus for the technical uddeśa/nirdeśa pair.

§19 — Grāhya-vikalpa = artha-sattva-pratibhāsa | Grāhaka-vikalpa = ātma-vijñapti-pratibhāsa VERIFIED

Yamaguchi text (almost entirely italicised reconstruction)

viśeṣataḥ tu grāhya-grāhaka-vikalpaḥ | tatra grāhya-vikalpaḥ artha-sattva-pratibhāsaṃ vijñānam | grāhaka- vikalpa ātma-vijñapti-pratibhāsam | dvayam grāhyaṃ grāhakaṃ ca iti tatra grāhyaṃ rūpādi | grāhakaṃ cakṣur-vijñānādi | grāhya-grāhaka-bhāvena rahitatā viviktatā hi abhūtaparikalpasya śūnyatā | na tu abhūtaparikalpo 'py abhāvāt | (p. 14; OCR lines 1563–1567)

Doctrinal placement

Sthiramati's grāhya/grāhaka taxonomy is from Madhyāntavibhāgabhāṣya ad MAV I.4 and from MSA XI.40 — the classical Yogācāra four-fold vikalpa scheme:

  • artha-pratibhāsa: appearance as object (rūpa etc.)
  • sattva-pratibhāsa: appearance as a sentient being (other minds)
  • ātma-pratibhāsa: appearance as a self
  • vijñapti-pratibhāsa: appearance as a cognition

Sthiramati groups them as grāhya-vikalpa (artha + sattva) and grāhaka-vikalpa (ātma + vijñapti). This is the standard MAV scheme cf. MAVbh local file lines 35–48 (the four-fold vikalpa).

Translation

"In particular, [the abhūta-parikalpa is] grāhya-vikalpa and grāhaka-vikalpa. Among these, grāhya-vikalpa is vijñāna appearing as object and as sentient-being; grāhaka- vikalpa is [vijñāna] appearing as self and as cognition. — [As kārikā I.1b says:] 'duality, [namely] grāhya and grāhaka' — among these, grāhya is rūpa etc., and grāhaka is cakṣur-vijñāna etc. The being-devoid-of, the isolation-from, the state-of-grāhya-and-grāhaka is the emptiness of the abhūta-parikalpa — not from the [supposed] absence of the abhūta-parikalpa itself."

Doctrinal placement — Ābdhātivādin reference

"Ābdhātivādin" reference The phrase ābdhātivādi-vat ("like the 'water-element-asserter'") refers to a Buddhist (Sautrāntika or Mahāyāna?) school holding that the pure ālaya-vijñāna / tathāgatagarbha is like the water-element: pervasive but unrecognised because covered by adventitious defilements. Cf. the parallel use in Sthiramati's other works and in the Ratnagotravibhāga tradition. The reference is brief here; Sthiramati signals familiarity rather than full citation.

The classical rajju-sarpa simile

"Just as a rope is empty of snake-nature (because it does not have that nature) — emptied of [snake-nature] at all times, but not empty of its own [rope-]nature — so here too." This is the canonical Yogācāra application of the rope-snake simile to the emptiness-of-duality / non-emptiness-of-abhūta-parikalpa dichotomy.

Translation

"Just as a rope is empty of snake-nature because it does not have that [snake-]nature — empty of it always — but not empty of rope-nature, so here too. — 'In it too, that [abhūta-parikalpa] is found' [I.1d] — i.e. the abhūta-parikalpa [is found in emptiness]. 'Thus, because [it] is defiled by adventitious obscurations, the non-apprehension [of emptiness] has been spoken of — as in the case of the Ābdhātivādin.' — Thus, 'one sees correctly: 'what is not in something, that is empty of that.'' What is not in what? — The duality is not in the abhūta-parikalpa. Hence one sees the abhūta-parikalpa as empty of duality."

§22 — Yat śūnyaṃ tasya sadbhāvāt — concluding articulation + alternative views VERIFIED

Yamaguchi text (italicised)

yat śūnyaṃ tasya sadbhāvāt | yena śūnyaṃ tasya tatra abhāvāt | sarva-bhāve sarva-abhāve tu na aviparītaṃ śūnyatā-lakṣaṇam | śūnyatāyāḥ eva abhāva-prasaṅgāt | na śūnya-saṃjñāyām asatyāṃ śūnyatā yogyā | dharmatāyāḥ hi bhāva-āyattatvāt anityatva-ādivat | (p. 14; OCR lines 1580–1584)

Structure

The crisp formulation of MAV's middle path:

  • yat śūnyaṃ tasya sadbhāvāt — "from the existence of that-which-is-empty [= abhūta-parikalpa]"
  • yena śūnyaṃ tasya tatra abhāvāt — "from the absence in it of that-by-which-it-is-empty [= duality]"

Wherein lies the non-distortedness. The opposing options: sarva-bhāva (all exists, no emptiness) or sarva-abhāva (all is non-existent) both fail. The reason: without something to be called empty, there is no emptiness — just as without dharmas, there is no impermanence etc. Dharmatā requires bhāva; it is dependent.

Translation

"[The lakṣaṇa is non-distorted] because of the presence of that-which-is-empty, and because of the absence-in-it of that-by-which-it-is-empty. — If [we said] 'all is existent' or 'all is non-existent', the lakṣaṇa of emptiness would not be non-distorted, because of the consequence that emptiness itself would not exist. For there is no fitness for emptiness if there is no [substrate] called empty — for dharma-nature is dependent on existents, like impermanence and so on."
