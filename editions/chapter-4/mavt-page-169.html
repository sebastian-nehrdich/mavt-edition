§42 MS fol. 57r7
Sanskrit
ātmābhiniveśavastu cittam iti | anyasya nityādi-guṇa-viśiṣṭasyātmano 'bhā-vād ātmābhi-niveśālambanaṃ cittam eva | tasmāt tatparīkṣayā nirodhasatyam avatarati śāntatvena |
Tibetan
bdag tu mngon par zhen [P117a1] pa'i gzhi ni sems te zhes bya ba la | rtag pa la sogs pa'i yon tan khyad par can gyi bdag gzhan med pas na bdag tu mngon par zhen pa'i dmigs pa ni sems nyid do || de bas [P117a2] na de [D271b6] yongs su brtags pas 'gog pa'i bden pa la zhi ba nyid du 'jug ste |
English
The basis for the stubborn clinging to a self is the mind
📝 noteIn the second chapter of the Madhyāntavibhāgaṭīkā (Sthiramati), Sthiramati discusses these bases of clining: Lack of skill with regard to the base is an obstacle with regard to the establishments of mindfulness. And these [establishments of mindfulness] are the four establishments of mindfulness of body, feeling, the mind and the phenomena. the bases of these [establishments of mindfulness] again are the body, feeling, the mind and the phenomena [respectively], because [the establishments of mindfulness] have these as their objects. Among these [bases], the basis for the stubborn clining to a [physical] foundation is the body. The basis for the stubborn clinging to enjoyment of the self is feeling. The basis for the stubborn clinging to a self is the mind. The basis to the stubborn clinging to the self as either defiled or pure are the phenomena.
Madhyāntavibhāgaṭīkā (Sthiramati) 90.14-19: smṛty upasthāneṣu vastvakauśalam āvaraṇam iti | tāni punaḥ kāyavedanācittadharmasmṛtyupasthānāni catvāri | teṣām punar vastu kāyavedanācittadharmāḥ tadviṣayatvāt | tatrāśrayābhiniveśavastu kāyaḥ | ātmaparibhogābhiniveśavastu vedanā | ātmābhiniveśavastu cittaṃ | ātmasaṃkleśavyavadānābhiniveśavastu dharmā[Y reads dharma. This improvement is suggested in St p. 117, fn. 171.] iti |
. Because of the absence of a different self that is characterized by the qualities such as being eternal and so on, only the mind is the basis for the stubborn clinging to a self. Therefore, by the investigation of it [the mind] one enters the truth of cessation as a state of peacefulness.
