§1 — Chapter-opening setup VERIFIED

Yamaguchi text (italics = reconstructed from Tibetan)

tattvapratipādanānantaraṁ pratipakṣabhāvanā vaktavyā | tatrāvasthā phalaprāptis ca | atas tatpratipadānārtham caturthaparicchedārambhaḥ | (p. 166; OCR lines 6803–6805)

Tibetan evidence

Source located: BO_T06_D4032 (Sthiramati MAVT in the Tengyur, Toh 4032). Chapter 4 opening at segments BO_T06_D4032:270a-0 through :270a-7.

དེ་ཁོ་ན་བཤད་པའི་འོག་ཏུ་གཉེན་པོ་བསྒོམ་པ་དང་། དེའི་གནས་དང་འབྲས་བུ་ཐོབ་པ་ བཤད་དགོས་ཏེ། དེ་བས་ན་དེ་བསྟན་པའི་ཕྱིར་ལེའུར་བཅད་པ་བཞི་པ་བརྩམས་སོ།

Gloss: "After reality has been explained, (i) the cultivation of counteragents, and (ii) its avasthā, and (iii) attainment of fruit must be explained; therefore, in order to teach those, the fourth chapter is undertaken." Note that the Tibetan has one verb (bshad dgos te, gerundive of obligation) governing three coordinated subjects — not three separate clauses as Yamaguchi's punctuation suggests.

Per-italics evidence and proposed re-readings

(a) *vaktavyā* → vaktavyāḥ (or alternatively vaktavyam)
The Tibetan bshad dgos te is a single gerundive governing all three subjects. Yamaguchi's singular vaktavyā agrees only with pratipakṣabhāvanā and leaves the next two subjects verbless. Plural vaktavyāḥ fits the three-fold nominative-feminine construction; the impersonal neuter vaktavyam is also possible (MS-attested at MAVT line 5143: lakṣaṇatattvānantaram aviparyāsatattvaṁ vaktavyam). The plural is preferable because the Tibetan back-reference de bas na de bstan pa'i phyir presupposes a plural antecedent ("teach those").
(b) *tatrāvasthā* *phalaprāptis* *ca* → tasyāś cāvasthā phalaprāptiś ca
The Tibetan de'i gnas dang 'bras bu thob pa is genitive (de'i = tasyāḥ, fem. sg. ablative/genitive back-reference to pratipakṣabhāvanā), not locative (tatra = de la / der). The decisive internal evidence is the very next sentence of the MAVT — itself MS-attested, not reconstructed — which reads tadavasthāphalaprāptyor uddeśaḥ (line 6806 of the source file): Sthiramati uses tad- (compounded) precisely where Yamaguchi here puts tatra. Re-reading as tasyāś cāvasthā phalaprāptiś ca coordinates the three subjects under one gerundive and matches both the Tibetan and the following MS-attested form. (Yamaguchi's three-daṇḍa punctuation should also be replaced by single coordinators.)
(c) *tatpratipadānārtham* → tatpratipādanārtham
This is a clear OCR / typesetting corruption: pratipadāna- is not a Sanskrit word, while pratipādana- ("expounding, causing to understand") is the standard nominal of pra-vi-pad causative. The MAVT itself has the form tatpratipādanārtham in three MS-attested (non-italic) locations: source-file lines 1671 (atas tatpratipādanārtham āha), 2204, and 3667 (tatpratipādanārtham ārabhyate). The Tibetan de bstan pa'i phyir is neutral on number and supports either tat- or teṣām; Sthiramati's established idiom across the MAVT consistently uses tat- (generic neuter back-reference). Verdict: mechanical correction to tatpratipādanārtham, not even a "variant" but a printing error.

Yamaguchi's Japanese translation

眞實示の次に對治の修習とそれの分位と得果とを説かざるべからず。 夫故にそれを説示せん爲に第四品の製作あり。 (references/mavt-ja.txt lines 3734–3735)

Translation

"After [the chapter on] the establishment of reality, [the chapter on] the cultivation of counteragents, together with its developmental stages (avasthā) and the attainment of its fruits (phalaprāpti), must be expounded; therefore, in order to expound those, the fourth chapter is undertaken."

Notes / open questions for the reviewer

  • vaktavyāḥ vs vaktavyam: the Tibetan does not disambiguate. Plural matches the three subjects more naturally; impersonal matches the MAVT-internal parallel at line 5143. Editor's call.
  • tasyāś ca vs tatra: the immediately following MS-attested compound tadavasthāphalaprāptyor (line 6806) decisively favours tad-/tasyāḥ over tatra. Yamaguchi's tatra appears to be a loose rendering of Tibetan de'i.
  • Punctuation: the Tibetan has one sentence with one gerundive. Yamaguchi's three-daṇḍa structure is a modernising over-segmentation not supported by the Tibetan.
  • tatpratipadāna → tatpratipādana: a clear printing / OCR error in Yamaguchi. No status as a "variant" — just a correction.

  • tattvānantaram uddiṣṭatvāt ↔ de kho na'i 'og tu bstan pa(s) … phyir — confirmed. Tibetan reads the ablative-of-cause as a single trailing phyir shared across both conjuncts.
  • pratipakṣabhāvanā-pratibaddhatvāt ↔ gnyen po bsgom pa la rag lus pa'i phyir — confirmed verbatim. rag las/lus pa is Sthiramati's standard gloss for pratibaddha- ("dependent on, bound to").

§3 — Sūtre pratipakṣatvena bodhipakṣya[ḥ] VERIFIED

Yamaguchi text (italics = reconstructed)

sūtre pratipakṣatvena bodhipakṣo 'numata iti pradarśanārtham āha | pratipakṣabhāvanā bodhipakṣabhāvanā iti | (p. 166; OCR lines 6810–6812)

The pivotal Vasubandhu source-quotation (MAVbh, MS-attested)

This whole sentence is Sthiramati's gloss on a single MAVbh sentence at the very opening of Vasubandhu's chapter 4 commentary — preserved in Sanskrit in Nagao's edition and indexed on DharmaMitra at MAVbh SA_T06_vmvkbh_u:789:

Vasubandhu, MAVbh ch. 4 opening (Nagao Mvbh 50) pratipakṣabhāvanā bodhipakṣyabhāvanā sedānīṃ vaktavyā / This is the direct lemma Sthiramati is sub-commenting on. Note the form bodhipakṣya- (with the -ya suffix), not Yamaguchi's bodhipakṣa-. Sthiramati's §3 quotes the equation half, and his §7 (sedānīm vaktavyā | tattvānantaram uddiṣṭatvāt) picks up the second half of the same MAVbh sentence.

Confirming evidence for -pakṣya- as the consistent MAVbh form: line 435 (bodhipakṣyeṣu tāvat /) and line 510 (tatvapraveśāvaraṇaṃ yad bodhipakṣyeṣu). The MAVT itself preserves bodhipakṣya in the MS at line 4670 (sarvabodhipakṣyadhar-, non-italic MS), and Yamaguchi's own footnote 4 to the present passage (OCR line 6833) records: Ms. bodhipakṣya — the second occurrence in §3 is in the MS and reads -pakṣya-. Yamaguchi silently normalised to -pakṣa-; this should be reverted.

Tibetan (BO_T06_D4032:270a-7)

བསྟན་པའི་མདོར་བྱས་པ་ལས་གཉེན་པོ་ནི་བྱང་ཆུབ་ཀྱི་ཕྱོགས་སུ་བཞེད་པར་ བསྟན་པའི་ཕྱིར། གཉེན་པོ་བསྒོམ་པ་ནི་བྱང་ཆུབ་ཀྱི་ཕྱོགས་བསྒོམ་པ་སྟེ་ཞེས་ བྱ་བ་སྨོས་སོ། །

Gloss: "In order to indicate that, in the brief teaching of the sūtra, the counteragent is held to be the bodhi-pakṣa, [Vasubandhu] says: 'cultivation of the counteragent is cultivation of the bodhi-pakṣa.'" The Tibetan calque byang chub kyi phyogs is neutral between -pakṣa- and -pakṣya-, but the standard Tib. for bodhipakṣya-dharma is byang chub kyi phyogs (dang mthun pa'i chos), which points to the -pakṣya- form.

Per-italics evidence and proposed re-readings

(a) bodhipakṣo → bodhipakṣyo
Three convergent witnesses: MAVbh (verbatim) reads bodhipakṣyabhāvanā; the MAVT MS itself (per Yamaguchi fn. 4) reads bodhipakṣya at the second occurrence; MAVT-internal non-italic at line 4670 has bodhipakṣyadharma-. Yamaguchi's silent normalisation to -pakṣa- is not supported by any witness.
(b) pratipakṣabhāvanā bodhipakṣabhāvanā iti → pratipakṣabhāvanā bodhipakṣyabhāvanā iti
The quoted formula is verbatim MAVbh ch. 4 opening (Nagao Mvbh 50; line 801). Sthiramati is quoting Vasubandhu directly; the wording must match.
(c) 'numata iti pradarśanārtham āha — confirmed in template, not in lemma
The frame iti pradarśanārtham āha is Sthiramati's standard "[in order to indicate X, he says Y]" idiom, abundantly attested in MAVT non-italic contexts (OCR lines 5150, 8575, 8584, 9314, 9804). The reconstruction is structurally sound; the only substantive correction needed inside it is bodhipakṣyo for bodhipakṣo (see (a)).
(d) minor: te hī → te hi
A typographic artefact in Yamaguchi's printing (the OCR may also be mis-reading); standard form is te hi.

The bracketing enumeration is a stock Abhidharma formula. Two witnesses:
  • Aṣṭādaśasāhasrikā-prajñāpāramitā (SA_K05_adsp70-u:3174): catvāri smṛtyupasthānāni. yāvad āryāṣṭāṅgaṃ mārgaṃ (acc. sg.).

Four items joined by dang: spel ba / bskyed pa / goms par bya ba / lan mang du bya ba — which fixes the Sanskrit parse as a four-item list:

  • bṛṁhaṇā (= spel ba, "increasing")
  • āropaṇā (= bskyed pa, lit. "installing, raising up" — the Tibetan renders the "raising" sense as "producing, generating")
  • abhyāsaḥ (= goms par bya ba, "habitual practice")
  • bahulā kriyā (= lan mang du bya ba, "doing many times")

Parse note

OCR bṛṁhaṇāropaṇā = sandhi for two items bṛṁhaṇā āropaṇā, not a single compound. The Tibetan four-item list disambiguates. No emendation; just a transliteration note for the apparatus.

Doctrinal placement and parallels (with dharmanexus URLs)

The Yogācāra stock-formula for the synonyms of bhāvanā is best known from the Śrāvakabhūmi, which uses a five-item list. Sthiramati's four-item list overlaps only partially with it; he keeps abhyāsaḥ and the bahulī- root, drops paricaya and āsevanā, and adds bṛṁhaṇā and āropaṇā. This is parallel in function but lexically distinct — not a citation.

Yogācārabhūmi, Śrāvakabhūmi (Nepalese edition) — SA_T06_srabhu_u:5771. The five-item stock formula: saptatriṃśatāṃ bodhipakṣyāṇāṃ dharmāṇām abhyāsaḥ paricaya āsevanā bhāvanā bahulīkāra iyam ucyate bodhipakṣya-bhāvanā

The MAVbh source — sentence-tail

The phrase sedānīṃ vaktavyā (sā idānīṃ vaktavyā, "[it] is now to be expounded") is the second half of the Vasubandhu MAVbh sentence whose first half Sthiramati glossed at §3. The complete MAVbh sentence is:

Sthiramati first glosses the equation half of this sentence in §3 (… bodhipakṣyabhāvanā iti), then in §7 picks up the tail (sedānīṃ vaktavyā) and gives the supporting reason (tattvānantaram uddiṣṭatvāt "because [in the piṇḍārtha] it is presented immediately after reality" — same connective as §2 and §1).

Yamaguchi's Japanese translation

四 念 處 何故に諸念の修習が初めに説かれ、諸餘の菩提分修習〔は初めに説かる〕に非るか。 (references/mavt-ja.txt line 3744)

Translation

"For what reason is precisely the cultivation of the smṛtyupasthānas [the foundations of mindfulness] taught first, and not some other cultivation of bodhipakṣa?"

Notes

  • This is Sthiramati's set-up question for the kārikā IV.1 quotation that follows (in §9 and over the page break to p. 167). The kārikā's first pāda (IV.1a) gives the doctrinal reason (dauṣṭhulya-tṛṣṇā-hetutvāt) and IV.1cd gives the soteriological reason (catuḥsatyāvatārāya).
  • The iva in smṛtyupasthāna-bhāvanā eva is the avadhāraṇa: "precisely (and not others)."

§9 — Āha: tatra tāvad ādau dauṣṭhulyāt tarṣa-hetutvāt — kārikā IV.1a pratīka VERIFIED

Yamaguchi text (italicised, reconstructed)

āha | tatra tāvad ādau dauṣṭhulyatṛṣṇāhetutvād (p. 166; OCR lines 6821–6822; continues on page 205 with iti vistaraḥ |)

The kārikā being cited (kārikā IV.1 in full)

Vasubandhu, MAV/MAVbh kārikā IV.1 (Nagao ed.) — MAVbh SA_T06_vmvkbh_u:794 ff.; indexed at SA_T06_vmvkbh_u:789. tatra tāvad ādau /
dauṣṭhulyāt tarṣa-hetutvād vastutvād avimohataḥ |
catuḥsatyāvatārāya smṛtyupasthānabhāvanā || IV.1 || "There, to begin with, at the outset — because of dysfunction (dauṣṭhulya), because of being the cause of craving (tarṣa-hetutva), because of [being] a thing (vastutva), and because [it brings] non-confusion (avimoha) — the cultivation of the foundations of mindfulness is for entry into the four [noble] truths." Four separate ablative-causes, each tied to one of the four smṛtyupasthānas (body / sensation / mind / dharmas) and one of the four noble truths.

Tibetan of kārikā IV.1 (MAVbh-Tib)

Vasubandhu MAVbh-Tib — BO_T06_D4027:15b-21. དེ་ལ་རེ་ཞིག་དང་པོར། ། གནས་ངན་ལེན་ཕྱིར་སྲེད་རྒྱུའི་ཕྱིར། ། གཞི་ཡི་ཕྱིར་དང་མ་རྨོངས་ཕྱིར། ། བདེན་པ་བཞི་ལ་འཇུག་བྱ་བས། ། དྲན་པ་ཉེ་བར་གཞག་པ་བསྒོམ། ། The Tibetan also has four discrete ablative members (gnas ngan len phyir / sred rgyu'i phyir / gzhi yi phyir / ma rmongs phyir) — i.e. it matches the kārikā literally and does not support a compound.

Sthiramati's own Tibetan handling of the kārikā

Sthiramati MAVT-Tib — BO_T06_D4032:270b-5 and :270b-11. Sthiramati's prose commentary at the §9 anchor first glosses gnas ngan len dang bcas pa'i 'du byed yin pa'i phyir (= sadauṣṭhulya-saṁskāratvāt), i.e. unpacks the first ablative dauṣṭhulyāt as a separate item; then moves on to tshor ba ni sred pa'i rgyu ste (= tṛṣṇā-hetur vedanā) at 270b-11 (which corresponds to Yamaguchi page 205 line 6896) as the second item. Decisive evidence that Sthiramati was glossing two separate kārikā-items in order, not a single compound.

Proposed re-reading

Read dauṣṭhulyāt tarṣa-hetutvād (two separate ablatives) for Yamaguchi's compound dauṣṭhulyatṛṣṇāhetutvād. Supporting evidence is unanimous: (i) the kārikā IV.1a as transmitted in MAVbh Sanskrit (Nagao ed.); (ii) the MAVbh Tibetan kārikā at BO_T06_D4027:15b-21 with four separated ablatives; (iii) Sthiramati's own Tibetan commentary which glosses the two items separately and in order; (iv) the structural argument: iti vistaraḥ at the head of page 205 is the standard MAVT marker for "the kārikā has just been cited by its opening words (a pratīka), with the rest understood" — incompatible with a Sanskrit compound, which is not a pratīka. Yamaguchi's reconstruction collapsed two kārikā items into one compound, likely misreading an abbreviated MS pratīka.
On tarṣa vs tṛṣṇā: keep tarṣa as the kārikā reading. Sthiramati's prose paraphrase uses tṛṣṇā-hetur vedanā (Yamaguchi p. 205 line 6896, confirmed by tshor ba ni sred pa'i rgyu ste in MAVT-Tib 270b-11), but that is a gloss, not a quotation. tarṣa is the kārikā form in both Nagao and Pandeya editions, and the Tibetan sred pa does not disambiguate tarṣa/tṛṣṇā.

Translation

"[Vasubandhu] says: 'There [in regard to that question], to begin with, at the outset — because of dysfunction (dauṣṭhulya), because of being the cause of craving (tarṣa-hetutva)' — [so the verse begins; the rest is to be understood at length, viz. the four ablatives followed by catuḥsatyāvatārāya smṛtyupasthāna-bhāvanā]."

Notes

  • This is the start of the kārikā IV.1a citation; the iti vistaraḥ on page 205 closes the abbreviated quotation and Sthiramati then commentates the four ablatives one by one over the following pages.
  • A further Sthiramati parallel — Sūtrālaṅkāravṛtti-bhāṣya BO_T06_D4034-2:104b-3 — paraphrases the same doctrine using equivalent Tibetan, confirming Sthiramati's consistent reading.
